# Phase 4C Information Scenario Consistency

Baseline: factual 2026 Phase 4A/4B layers
Horizon states: A2050, A2075, B2050, B2075, C2050, C2075

All records in the Phase 4C tables are separate fictional scenario deltas. No
scenario has a probability, deterministic forecast, future coordinate, or claim
to replace the frozen 2026 baseline.

## Scenario A — Trusted Public Infrastructure

### A2050 — Managed interoperability

DRIVERS: Coordinated observing systems, stronger provenance, shared models, and
public explanation.

ASSUMPTIONS: Selected water, weather, regulatory, and energy products become
interoperable; source lineage and public explanations improve; City, NWS, EPA/
state, and PJM retain human authority.

PERSISTING SYSTEMS: GLERL, GLOS, NOAA forecasts, Toledo water operations, NWPS,
NWS, EPA ECHO, EIA, and PJM remain recognizable baseline interfaces.

ROLE CHANGES: GLOS/ECHO products gain stronger cataloging and provenance; NOAA
forecast and NWS explanation roles expand; public access broadens selectively.

NEW FUNCTIONS: Regional provenance/catalog, model interoperability, and public
decision explanation.

GOVERNANCE MODEL: Interoperable coordination with human institutional authority.

INFORMATION DEPENDENCIES: Shared data and explanations reduce some handoff and
interpretation gaps but increase dependence on standards and maintenance.

BLIND-SPOT CHANGES: Spatial coverage and warning-timing gaps are reduced;
forecast and regulatory gaps are transformed rather than eliminated; public
energy operational gaps persist; institutional complexity is new.

SECURITY / TRUST TRADEOFFS: Openness and provenance improve trust while
operational information remains selectively restricted.

FAILURE MODES: Broken standards, stale catalogs, incompatible models, and
accountability disputes weaken the pathway.

UNCERTAINTIES: The degree of institutional investment, adoption, and public
trust is unknown.

WHAT WOULD WEAKEN THE SCENARIO: Fragmented standards, weak provenance, or
failure to maintain shared public products.

### A2075 — Mature trusted infrastructure

A2075 extends the same family into mature interoperable public information. Most
major products are traceable and comparable, while jurisdiction, protected
operator information, and uneven institutional trust remain. The scenario
reduces several baseline gaps but does not create complete lake coverage, one
regulator, or universal transparency.

## Scenario B — Federated Resilience

### B2050 — Distributed federation

DRIVERS: Distributed ownership, local processing, selective exchange, privacy-
aware stewardship, and graceful degradation.

ASSUMPTIONS: Municipalities, agencies, reporters, and operators retain distinct
systems; selected records are exchanged; local decisions remain possible when a
central product is unavailable.

PERSISTING SYSTEMS: Phase 4A/4B observations, forecasts, regulatory interfaces,
and PJM/EIA public information remain the named reference points.

ROLE CHANGES: GLOS, NOAA, NWS, EPA/ECHO, and PJM become federation participants;
data exchange becomes selective rather than universally open.

NEW FUNCTIONS: Local edge analysis, federated exchange agreements, and
continuity/redundancy support.

GOVERNANCE MODEL: Federated authority with local autonomy and cross-checking.

INFORMATION DEPENDENCIES: Multiple observation and model paths reduce central-
service dependence but increase coordination and standard divergence.

BLIND-SPOT CHANGES: Central-service and selected timing gaps are reduced;
station-to-forecast provenance and jurisdictional handoffs are transformed;
data fragmentation is new.

SECURITY / TRUST TRADEOFFS: Selective sharing and privacy-aware stewardship
improve local control but constrain interoperability and shared interpretation.

FAILURE MODES: Local systems diverge, federation agreements lapse, or multiple
models disagree without a common resolution process.

UNCERTAINTIES: The scale of local capability and the quality of federation are
exploratory.

WHAT WOULD WEAKEN THE SCENARIO: Poor interoperability, insufficient local
maintenance, or inability to coordinate across jurisdictions.

### B2075 — Mature federated resilience

B2075 assumes durable local/regional federation, multiple fallback paths, and
independent model checking. Local autonomy remains strong; regional synthesis is
slower or contested when standards, provenance, or authority differ.

## Scenario C — High-Automation / Contested Information

### C2050 — Accelerating automation

DRIVERS: Dense sensing, AI-assisted analysis, strategic information value,
compute dependence, and stronger authentication.

ASSUMPTIONS: AI assists HAB and flood forecasting, anomaly detection expands,
operator data become more interconnected, and operational access becomes more
restricted. City, NWS/public authorities, EPA/state authorities, and PJM retain
human final authority.

PERSISTING SYSTEMS: Phase 4A/4B water, hydrology, regulatory, and energy
interfaces remain the factual anchors.

ROLE CHANGES: NOAA/NWS forecasts gain AI-assisted analysis; EPA/ECHO and PJM
products gain stronger provenance or access controls; operator-led automation is
bounded by human supervision.

NEW FUNCTIONS: Cross-domain AI analysis, automated anomaly detection, and
provenance/authentication assurance.

GOVERNANCE MODEL: Operator-led automation with explicit human supervisory and
final decision authority.

INFORMATION DEPENDENCIES: Speed and analytical density rise alongside model,
compute, availability, provenance, and access dependence.

BLIND-SPOT CHANGES: Forecast, timing, regulatory, and energy gaps are transformed
into opacity, contestation, restricted access, and concentration concerns; a
model-opacity/trust gap is new.

SECURITY / TRUST TRADEOFFS: Authentication, provenance, and access control
strengthen integrity while opacity and operational separation increase.

FAILURE MODES: Model disagreement, stale data, compute unavailability, opaque
recommendations, and contested institutional legitimacy weaken decisions.

UNCERTAINTIES: This is the most speculative family; no specific AI system,
capacity, or operational automation is forecast.

WHAT WOULD WEAKEN THE SCENARIO: Failure of investment, weak human oversight,
poor provenance, or public rejection of machine-assisted decisions.

### C2075 — Mature contested automation

C2075 assumes dense interconnected information infrastructure, routine AI-assisted
analysis, bounded automated continuity, and persistent contestation over model
legitimacy. Human authority remains explicit. Higher automation does not create
sovereign AI, a single trusted model, or public access to sensitive operations.

## Cross-scenario consistency

- All six states retain the four Phase 4A/4B representative chains.
- All future objects are fictional scenario deltas with explicit assumptions.
- Human decision authority remains explicit in every family.
- A emphasizes openness and provenance; B emphasizes federation and local
  resilience; C emphasizes speed, automation, concentration, and contested trust.
- No scenario changes the five-region canon or modifies the factual 2026 tables.
