# Ecological Dependency Assumptions

Phase 6B is a factual 2026 qualitative dependency, disturbance, and resilience layer over Phase 6A. It is not a quantitative ecological-risk assessment, population model, extinction forecast, conservation ranking, economic valuation, disease model, or future scenario.

## Qualitative classes

`low`, `moderate`, `high`, and `unknown` describe relative evidence-qualified dependence or system support. They are not probabilities, effect sizes, vulnerability scores, or forecasts. `unknown` is retained separately from `low` where redundancy, condition, or substitution is not documented.

## Dependency semantics

Dependencies connect ecological functions to habitat, hydrology, wetland, riparian, migration, food-web, landscape, protected-area, or restoration context. A dependency edge is an analytical relationship; it is not a literal animal route. Relationship basis and confidence remain explicit.

## Disturbance semantics

The disturbance register records documented or qualified presence of broad pressures. It does not quantify exposure, consequence, attribution, trend, or species-specific impact. `evidence_status` distinguishes documented context from qualified context.

## Resilience semantics

The resilience matrix describes visible habitat redundancy, connectivity, protected support, restoration capacity, mobility, monitoring, and disturbance exposure. It is descriptive system resilience, not a risk score or ranking.

## Black Swamp and sensitive-location policy

The Great Black Swamp geometry candidate remains **C — HOLD / noncanonical** and is not used as a dependency boundary. Sensitive species locations, nests, roosts, dens, rare occurrences, and precise spawning sites are excluded. No exact movement routes are drawn.
