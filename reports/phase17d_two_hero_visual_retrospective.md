## What the two heroes establish

The strongest shared invention is **a future system with a maintainable section**. HF-01's roof is deep enough for shade, attachment, access, and passage; HF-04's interior is deep enough for testing, holding, repair, utilities, and separation. These are legible spatial propositions before they are explanations of specific technology. The selective transparency is functional: glass admits or filters light at the street and protects observation at the plant. Opacity is equally necessary for machinery, privacy, isolation, and material storage. This is a more useful house rule than a prescribed amount of glass.

The second durable rule is **an observable limit**. HF-01's opening and mismatched leaves imply interrupted ownership, wear, and replacement. HF-04's empty berth and hold/reject bay interrupt the false story that a connected system always works. Yet the limits are unevenly visible: HF-01's property boundary and HF-04's material decision become explicit chiefly through the SVG and page text. That is acceptable for a three-part editorial sequence, provided future wide images do not claim to explain every process at once. The hero should attract; the close view should make work inspectable; the inset should name relationships and non-claims.

The pair also establishes a disciplined kind of spectacle. The large canopy and cell stack visibly change a street and a hall. They are spectacular because they occupy space and alter work, while the pages state their S/K status. Keep ordinary people at a believable scale and give at least one person a task with consequences: guiding a repair, holding material, deciding access, recovering equipment, or interpreting a sample. A person placed only to signal scale is insufficient. Conversely, a small image cannot visibly encode a whole institution; the caption and inset must carry the boundary between a worker's action and an authorized decision.

## What should change in the next visual experiment

The most consequential repetition is compositional, not merely material. Both wide images have dark inherited masonry to the left, a lifted replacement piece near the center, bright distance beyond, workers in high-visibility clothing, and wet reflective ground. Each arrangement makes sense on its own. Together, they risk teaching that Glass Basin means the same industrial theater rendered with a different object. The next experiment should preserve **repairability**, not another dangling module; preserve **weather**, not another amber storm break; preserve **inherited place**, not mandatory brick. Mud, reeds, winter spray, field dust, crop tissue, plastic film, worn seals, and corroded shore equipment can carry time just as well.

Avoid a mandatory palette or camera recipe. HF-01 succeeds with a low public viewpoint under a lateral canopy. HF-04 succeeds with the open hall framing a vertical frontage. A new hero should choose its viewpoint from the landscape and primary work, not from either accepted image. It should also test a different human distribution: a farmer at a ditch gate, a crew recovering an instrument, or a resident questioning a public notice. The people need not all be posed around one heroic replacement event.

## Next hero: production-order decision

**Recommend HF-03 — Maumee Bio-Ag Landscape as the next *visual production experiment*.** This is an order of testing, not a ranking of futures or an authorization to render. HF-03 asks whether the established grammar survives outside brick-and-steel Toledo: a long folded cultivation/processing seam against fields, a muddy ditch and older gate, a machine service berth, a qualified or held biological/material interface, and wetland margins that remain separate from production. It can test whether repair, functional translucency, outside inputs, human judgment, and uncertainty are recognizable in clay, crop, drainage, robotics, and contained biology. The accepted concept supplies those elements; the experiment must not claim yield, nutrient removal, ecological benefit, safety, or environmental release.

HF-02 is a strong alternative and offers the clearest open-water horizon, weather exposure, and recovery operation. Its lake instrument cradle and service terrace could break the enclosed-hall camera, but a crane or recovered craft suspended at an old concrete edge could repeat the current lift motif. It is the next fallback if HF-03 cannot achieve a legible landscape-scale invention. HF-05 poses an important civic question about observation and authority; visually, its retained brick hall, stacked instrument wall, and withdrawn module are closest to HF-04. Producing it immediately would be a weaker test of the house style's range.

The first HF-03 gate should be **one composition study**, reviewed at banner and 390-pixel card scale before any final set is commissioned. It should read as productive fields → maintained seam → ditch/wetland edge, with a distinct agricultural repair task and ordinary outside supply. Review whether cultivation, biological qualification, drainage, and stewardship remain separable; whether the held interface is visible without making the wetland look like a treatment claim; and whether a viewer can recognize Glass Basin without brick, a port crane, or a suspended box. A failed study should return to composition, not receive promotional finish.
