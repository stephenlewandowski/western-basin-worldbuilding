# Climate across the Western Basin world

**5 October 2026 · scenario/design amendments; existing science and geometry retained**

Climate modifies operating conditions and human choices. The following proposals
establish no performance, deployment, universal service or new canon.
Evidence and data boundaries: [climatology/readiness report](climate_integration_2026-10-05.md).

| Place / system | Human thermal environment | Lake / water | Energy / built environment | Air quality | Ordinary action / visual amendment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glass City 2075 | Continuous shade from transit to passage, repair and rest; exposed pavement; warm-night recovery | Nearby water does not guarantee local cooling | Solar control, glare, ventilation, envelope, refuge and condensate path; repair can interrupt shade | Filtered indoor refuge may constrain open-window ventilation | Move a queue or stool out of a missing panel's sun patch; schedule repair early |
| Lake Erie Energy & Security Coast | Exposed quay versus covered bench; heat and wind are separate | Variable ice and heat storage; episodic western stratification; depth/oxygen and central-basin advection separate from surface averages | Community, compute and industry cooling compete for capacity; conditional storage/load management/intake checks | Wind, ozone and smoke information can change open-bay use | Compare a depth recorder and surface record; return a case before a shade break |
| Maumee Bio-Ag 2075 | Field heat, shade/water/rest; warm-night recovery; task-appropriate PPE | Evapotranspiration, crop heat, drought versus drainage, extreme rainfall; no automatic lake HAB outcome | Pumping allocations, enclosure shading/ventilation, soil-water conservation; pest/disease seasons need further evidence | Poor-air advisories can restrict field work; fields are not guaranteed clean air | Clear a gate after rain, then conserve water in a dry interval; shift maintenance to dawn |
| Industrial Metabolism 2075 | Radiant/process heat differs from outdoor air; protected work and cooled breaks | Cooling-water dependency only where actually connected; no invented universal lake plumbing | Distinguish rejected/recovered heat, chilled loops, storage; only eligible cycles can shift during peaks | Local extraction/filtration versus open-door ventilation depends on process contaminants | A shift lead delays a discretionary cycle while essential work continues |
| Basin Reading Hall | Public comfort, neighborhood observations, heat/cooling information; no universal refuge promise | Surface/depth/ice records answer different questions | People, instrument rooms, compute and preservation need separate conditioning; plant/service behind rooms | Airport, shaded sensor and loading-area representativeness/privacy; ventilation may need filtering | Ask which temperature describes a work shift; keep counter front-left, return left-rear, spine right |
| Field-to-Lake / watershed accounting | Field-crew exposure and sampling windows | Climate alters timing and receiving-water context; routing alone cannot predict thermal structure, oxygen or blooms | Sampling/pumping depend on power/access | Smoke/ozone may affect field access | Compare a dated observation with a missing interval |
| Intake Crib study | Exposed access and work windows | Surface, depth, wind, oxygen and ice remain separate | Monitoring may inform intake decisions; no guaranteed treatment/cooling performance | Weather/air restrict access without supplying measurements | Ask why a lake-wide average does not answer at one intake depth |
| Farm / Industrial Exchange studies | Work/rest and seasonal operating constraints | Drought/rain and conditional cooling interfaces modify existing decisions | Conservation and qualified heat exchange retain their original limits | Air and ventilation add operating constraints | Show a delayed task and a maintained service route; retain the synthetic spatial studies |
| Energy / grid / compute baseline | Access to cooling differs among communities | Thermal rejection and water availability require site-specific evidence | Climate raises cooling pressure; generation, storage, distribution and loads constrain response | Filtration has its own energy/maintenance cost | Separate who can delay work from who still needs a cooled room |
| Model Lab / measured river track | Monitoring is dated work by people | Future boundary conditions need compatible observations; no temperature-to-nutrient shortcut | No arithmetic or model-case change | No air-quality inference from flow | Keep observed flow, synthetic routing and future scenario conditions visibly distinct |

Evidence for cooling/grid and air-quality coupling comes from
[NCA5 Energy](https://nca2023.globalchange.gov/chapter/5/) and
[NCA5 Midwest](https://nca2023.globalchange.gov/chapter/24/). Lake qualifications
come from [NOAA GLERL](https://www.glerl.noaa.gov/data/ice/) and the
[Pigeon Bay observations](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jglr.2025.102596).
All proposed responses and scene actions remain speculative.
