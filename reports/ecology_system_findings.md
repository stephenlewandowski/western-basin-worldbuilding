# Ecology System Findings

## Western Lake Erie aquatic system

The western basin, Maumee Bay, and the Maumee River form a connected aquatic and nearshore structure. HAB and nutrient conditions are represented as ecological-condition interfaces, not as a current bloom footprint or quantified causal model. Walleye, yellow perch, broader fish assemblages, plankton, benthos, and cyanobacteria are representative functions rather than stock or abundance series.

## Maumee, tributaries, and floodplain

Existing physical hydrography provides the geographic skeleton for river, tributary, riparian, and floodplain interfaces. The model supports broad hydrologic and habitat connectivity but does not infer animal travel paths or fish passage performance.

## Coastal wetlands and marshes

The generalized contemporary NWI network, Ottawa National Wildlife Refuge, Maumee Bay, and coastal restoration context form the strongest wetland and migratory-bird structure in the package. The NWI count is an inventory context measure, not total wetland area or condition.

## Black Swamp legacy and agricultural matrix

Modern drainage-altered agriculture, wetland remnants, riparian habitat, and restoration context carry the historical Black Swamp legacy. The held historical polygon is not canonicalized and is not used as a 2026 habitat boundary.

## Terrestrial habitat and fragmentation

The baseline uses generalized contemporary classes—forest, grassland, wetland, agriculture, developed land, and riparian habitat—to frame a terrestrial mosaic. Developed edges are shown only as broad fragmentation context; no ecological harm or causation is assigned from proximity.

## Migratory and mobile functions

Coastal wetlands support a broad migratory-bird stopover function. Lake Erie–tributary fish movement is represented as a generalized ecological function. Pollinator habitat is represented at broad habitat-mosaic level only. No exact routes or sensitive occurrences are exposed.

## Cross-system implication

Ecology cuts across the five interpretive macroregions. Water structure, nutrient/HAB conditions, freight/urban interfaces, agricultural transformation, and public observation systems provide later analytical entry points, but Phase 6A does not model ecological dependency, disturbance risk, resilience, or future change.

## Principal gaps

High-value next evidence includes consistent contemporary land-cover processing, wetland condition and restoration attribution, tributary-specific fish habitat evidence, aggregated migratory-bird observations, and defensible biodiversity indicators. These remain future work and are not resolved here.
