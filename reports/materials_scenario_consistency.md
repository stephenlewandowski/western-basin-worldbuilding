# Phase 2D Materials Scenario Consistency

All six states are fictional deltas over the immutable 2026 graph. They are
neither forecasts nor probabilities. The formal 30-record ledger is
`data/processed/scenarios/materials_scenario_assumptions.csv`.

## Scenario A — Continuity / Strategic Resilience

**Drivers:** supply resilience, incremental efficiency, process assurance, and
selective recycling. **Assumptions:** existing carbonate and Elmore capabilities
remain valuable; infrastructure modernizes without a revolutionary technology.
**Persisting nodes:** Woodville, Genoa, Elmore, water-treatment functions, and
freight interfaces. **New functions:** traceability and strategic-material
recovery. **New relationships:** incremental recovery, assurance, water, and
logistics interfaces. **Constraints:** no local beryllium mining, megafactory,
regional fusion dominance, route, or quantity. **Failure modes:** facility
attrition, weak demand, energy cost, or policy reversal. **Uncertainties:** no
facility survival or expansion footprint is guaranteed. **Would weaken it:**
loss of regional processing capacity or sustained failure to diversify supply.

In 2050 the change is incremental. By 2075 persistence becomes more uncertain,
while recycling and traceability mature if investment continues.

## Scenario B — Circular Industrial Basin

**Drivers:** carbon constraints, water stress, material scarcity, and waste
costs. **Assumptions:** secondary feedstocks, low-carbon calcination, industrial
water reuse, and reverse logistics become coordinated. **Persisting nodes:**
Woodville, Genoa, and Elmore change function rather than simply grow. **New
functions:** recovery exchange, water loop, carbon accounting/mineralization,
and Luckey remediation knowledge. **New relationships:** waste-to-feedstock,
water reuse, reverse logistics, and knowledge transfer. **Constraints:** no real
commercial symbiosis is claimed. **Failure modes:** contaminated feedstocks,
coordination failure, high energy cost, or weak markets. **Uncertainties:**
technology mix and governance. **Would weaken it:** low virgin-material prices,
fragmented regulation, or reuse systems that cost more water/energy than they save.

In 2050 enabling infrastructure expands. By 2075 closed-loop integration is
stronger but more dependent on shared standards and governance.

## Scenario C — High-Convergence Glass Basin

**Drivers:** faster fusion progress, photonics/technical-glass demand, advanced
ceramics, robotics, and AI-enabled manufacturing. **Assumptions:** existing
glass and strategic-processing skills become unusually valuable without making
the region a universal technology capital. **Persisting nodes:** Elmore and
carbonate processing expand roles; Luckey remains legacy/observation, never
revived production. **New functions:** technical glass/photonics, validated
digital twins, fusion-material demand, and high-value recovery. **New
relationships:** strategic material, compute/automation, high-reliability heat,
and recovery interfaces. **Constraints:** no Toledo fusion plant, named future
customer, output quantity, or precise new facility. **Failure modes:** fusion
delay, material substitution, grid constraint, cyber risk, or skills shortage.
**Uncertainties:** highest of the three scenarios. **Would weaken it:** failure
to close fusion materials gaps or inability to provide reliable power and water.

In 2050 convergence is emerging. By 2075 it is mature only under multiple
simultaneous exploratory assumptions.

## Materials Corridor test

**B — CORRIDOR EMERGES WEAKLY.** Scenarios B and C repeatedly connect
Woodville/Genoa carbonate roles, Elmore processing, Luckey knowledge, and Toledo-
area functions through shared recovery, water, energy, freight, and automation
interfaces. Scenario A is better described as a distributed network. No state
contains a documented transport route or direct interfacility flow, so a hard
polygon or administrative “corridor” would mislead. Map 10 uses networks only.
