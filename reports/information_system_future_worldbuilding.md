# Information System Future Worldbuilding

## 2026 FACTS

Phase 4A and Phase 4B establish the factual project baseline: selected public
observation stations and networks, public data products and forecasts,
decision organizations, operational interfaces, information dependencies, blind
spots, and authority boundaries. The baseline includes uncertainty around the
Toledo intake coordinate, representative point coverage, public-data limits,
organizational handoffs, and internal operator detail.

The accepted/frozen Phase 4A and Phase 4B artifacts remain immutable. Phase 4C
does not correct, overwrite, or reinterpret them as future facts.

## 2050 / 2075 SCENARIOS

### Trusted Public Infrastructure

Information behaves more like public infrastructure: interoperable, traceable,
explainable, and broadly useful. The civic tension is openness versus protected
operations and institutional complexity. The City, NWS/public authorities,
EPA/state authorities, and PJM retain human authority even where models and
catalogs are shared.

### Federated Resilience

The region favors local capability, distributed ownership, selective exchange,
redundant paths, and graceful degradation. The civic tension is resilience and
autonomy versus fragmentation and incompatible standards. A locally useful
model may be less legible to a regional coordinator.

### High-Automation / Contested Information

Dense sensing, AI-assisted analysis, machine-to-machine exchange, and bounded
automation make information faster and more valuable. The civic tension is
speed versus opacity, concentration, access restriction, model dependence, and
legitimacy. AI assists analysis and operations in bounded ways; it does not
receive sovereign or final governmental authority.

## Potential themes

- who controls environmental truth;
- trusted forecasts versus contested forecasts;
- public transparency versus operational security;
- algorithmic recommendation versus human authority;
- sensor-rich but interpretation-poor environments;
- local autonomy versus regional coordination;
- data provenance as civic infrastructure;
- institutional trust and model disagreement;
- information rationing and public/operational boundaries;
- digital continuity during disasters;
- data stewardship and sovereignty; and
- unequal access to high-quality information.

These are explicitly speculative interpretations, not findings about current
organizations or future facts.

## Boundaries

Phase 4C does not model exploit paths, offensive cyber operations, sensitive
facility layouts, SCADA, credentials, target prioritization, detailed privacy
impacts, military monitoring, or a future technical architecture. Security is
limited to high-level integrity, availability, authentication, provenance,
access, redundancy, continuity, trust, and concentration concepts.

Phase 4C is the final phase authorized by this run. Phase 4D or another future
scenario phase is not approved.
