# Ecological Dependency Findings

## Western Lake Erie

Aquatic function is concentrated around the lake–bay–tributary system. Nutrient and HAB conditions are important disturbance interfaces, while fish and food-web relationships remain broad and qualitative. No current consequence or stock condition is calculated.

## Maumee and floodplain

The strongest dependency is hydrologic: tributary, river, riparian, and floodplain functions rely on connected mapped water structure. Drainage-altered hydrology constrains the system, but no ditch-level or parcel-level causal claim is made.

## Coastal wetlands

Wetland function depends on the existing contemporary wetland network and coastal connectivity. Ottawa refuge context supplies protected-area support. Condition, redundancy, and restoration performance remain qualified.

## Black Swamp legacy

The modern agricultural matrix carries a legacy of drainage alteration and wetland reduction. The historical Great Black Swamp candidate is not used as a factual ecological boundary.

## Terrestrial fragmentation

Developed edges and a working agricultural landscape constrain broad terrestrial and riparian continuity. These are landscape interfaces, not quantified habitat loss or conservation-priority rankings.

## Migratory/mobile functions

Migratory-bird stopover and fish lake-to-tributary movement depend on habitat and connectivity. Mobility offers some broad functional redundancy, but exact routes and population series are absent. Pollinator function is represented only as a generalized habitat-mosaic dependency.

## Resilience

Coastal protected support and mobile-species function provide the clearest qualitative resilience signals. Hydrologic structure is strong at mapped-network level, while habitat redundancy, restoration capacity, terrestrial connectivity, and disturbance exposure remain mixed or uncertain.

## Principal gaps

The highest-value gaps are wetland condition, restoration attribution, tributary-specific habitat evidence, consistent contemporary land-cover processing, aggregated mobile-species observations, and defensible measures of ecological redundancy. These are not resolved in Phase 6B.
