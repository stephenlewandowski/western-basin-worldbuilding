# Materials System Future Worldbuilding

## 2026 Baseline

Fact: Northwest Ohio has local carbonate extraction/processing, Elmore advanced
strategic-material processing using nonlocal beryllium feed, and Luckey legacy
remediation. The baseline remains 30 nodes and 31 relationships.

## Scenario A — Strategic Resilience

Speculation: continuity makes maintenance, traceability, specialized labor, and
selective recycling strategic assets. Its advantage is lower transition risk;
its cost is continued dependence on external feedstocks and infrastructure.

## Scenario B — Circular Industrial Basin

Speculation: value shifts from extraction volume toward recovery, low-carbon
processes, water reuse, reverse logistics, and institutional coordination. Its
advantage is resilience; its cost is technical and governance complexity.

## Scenario C — High-Convergence Glass Basin

Speculation: glass, optics, strategic processing, robotics, and validated digital
manufacturing converge as fusion and other high-temperature technologies mature.
Its advantage is high-value capability; its costs are energy, water, security,
skills, and dependence on uncertain technology trajectories.

## 2050 Differences

Scenario A emphasizes incremental expansion; B builds recovery/reuse functions;
C establishes emerging technical-glass, fusion-material, and digital-production
interfaces. All new functions remain generalized and non-geocoded.

## 2075 Differences

A matures slowly and may lose facilities; B becomes a coordinated closed-loop
system; C becomes a dense high-value ecosystem only if its compound assumptions
hold. Progress is not treated as linear or inevitable.

## Materials Corridor Finding

The analytical result is **B — weak emergence** in B/C, not a factual corridor.
It is a network label only, never a polygon or sixth macroregion.

## Regional Tradeoffs

Continuity trades innovation speed for lower disruption. Circularity trades
waste reduction for coordination burdens. Convergence trades high-value work for
greater energy, water, security, and supply-chain exposure.

## Story Engines

Speculative story engines include resource nationalism, recycling bottlenecks,
water allocation, cleanup versus redevelopment, labor/automation conflict,
industrial secrecy, energy scarcity, cyber compromise of material passports,
and disputes over whether monitoring knowledge creates public value or private
control. None is a 2026 factual claim.
