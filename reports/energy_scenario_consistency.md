# Phase 3C Energy Scenario Consistency

## Scope and fixed baseline

2026 remains factual: Phase 3A is the frozen 18-node/17-edge energy baseline and
Phase 3B is the accepted/frozen 5-node/28-edge qualitative dependency layer.
The six states below are separate scenario deltas. They do not overwrite a
2026 row, create a power-flow model, assign future MW, or assert a probability.

Every state contains six ledger assumptions, 16 baseline-asset states, and three
generalized new-function nodes. `PERSISTS`, `EXPANDS ROLE`, `CHANGES FUNCTION`,
`DECLINES`, `RETIRES`, and `UNKNOWN` describe narrative/system roles, not
forecasts.

## A2050 — Managed Transition

**Drivers:** planned Monroe retirement, incremental grid modernization, gradual
industrial electrification, and measured compute growth.

**Assumptions:** nuclear persists; gas declines rather than vanishing; wind,
solar, and storage expand; critical water functions gain incremental resilience;
Elmore expands while Woodville changes process function.

**Persisting assets:** Davis-Besse, Fermi 2, and the existing named generation,
storage, water, materials, and compute anchors remain reference points.

**Role changes:** Monroe retires in the scenario; gas roles decline; renewable
and storage roles expand; Collins Park and Bay View change toward resilience;
Oppidan is an expanding regional/existing-site role, not a guaranteed campus.

**New functions:** generalized grid modernization, critical-load resilience, and
industrial/compute convergence functions.

**Dependency changes:** fuel dependence decreases; cooling and electricity
remain important; storage and coordination provide incremental support.

**Constraints / failure modes:** transition timing, permitting, fuel markets,
weather variability, and storage limits can slow the pathway. No replacement
technology or capacity deficit is calculated.

**Uncertainties / weakening evidence:** a material change in Monroe's status,
nuclear licensing, industrial investment, or compute commitments would weaken
this state.

## A2075 — Managed Transition

**Drivers:** mature efficiency and flexibility with lower external-fuel reliance.

**Assumptions:** Monroe and most gas roles retire/decline; renewables and
storage expand; nuclear license outcomes remain unknown; water and materials
functions become more efficient; compute remains generalized.

**Persisting assets:** regional interfaces, renewable anchors, storage functions,
and critical water/material services remain legible even where individual
generation assets are uncertain.

**Role changes:** Davis-Besse and Fermi 2 are `UNKNOWN`, not assumed extended;
Collins Park, Bay View, and Woodville change function; Elmore and compute expand
without a quantitative load claim.

**New functions:** mature coordination, resilience, and industrial/compute
service roles.

**Dependency changes:** fuel dependence falls; weather and storage remain visible;
water-energy coupling is reduced qualitatively through efficiency and reuse.

**Constraints / failure modes:** long-lived assets, capital turnover, drought or
extreme weather, and uncertain compute scale could interrupt the pathway.

**Uncertainties / weakening evidence:** a new firm-generation buildout or a
large unverified compute commitment would weaken the managed, lower-fuel reading.

## B2050 — Distributed Resilience

**Drivers:** critical-load resilience, distributed support, flexible demand,
storage deployment, and industrial efficiency.

**Assumptions:** nuclear persists as one possible firm resource; Monroe retires;
gas declines; renewable and storage roles expand; water and materials sites gain
flexibility; compute participates in demand management rather than dominating.

**Persisting assets:** Davis-Besse, Fermi 2, renewable anchors, storage assets,
and named water/material loads remain baseline reference points.

**Role changes:** Collins Park, Bay View, Elmore, Woodville, and Oppidan change
function toward flexible/resilient operation; gas and Monroe decline/retire.

**New functions:** distributed grid modernization, critical-load resilience, and
flexible industrial/compute services.

**Dependency changes:** electricity, fuel, cooling, and compute sensitivities are
qualitatively reduced at selected interfaces; storage support increases but is
finite.

**Constraints / failure modes:** coordination complexity, local maintenance,
weather correlation, cyber/communications disruption, and finite storage can
defeat the resilience promise. No islanding guarantee is made.

**Uncertainties / weakening evidence:** absent local flexibility programs,
backup specifications, or storage deployment evidence would weaken this state.

## B2075 — Distributed Resilience

**Drivers:** mature distributed support, demand flexibility, water-system
resilience, and industrial efficiency.

**Assumptions:** Davis-Besse persists as a possible regional firm anchor while
Fermi 2 remains unknown; Monroe and gas roles retire/decline; renewables and
storage expand; compute grows but stays flexible.

**Persisting assets:** the regional interface concept, distributed renewable
functions, storage support, and critical services persist even when individual
plants are uncertain.

**Role changes:** Collins Park and Bay View change function; Elmore and Woodville
become more efficient/circular; Oppidan becomes an expanding flexible service.

**New functions:** mature grid coordination, critical-load support, and flexible
industrial/compute functions.

**Dependency changes:** lower external-fuel and direct single-interface reliance;
higher storage and communications coordination reliance.

**Constraints / failure modes:** local autonomy can fragment coordination;
storage duration, replacement parts, and severe weather remain limiting factors.

**Uncertainties / weakening evidence:** no evidence of distributed deployment,
flexible tariffs, or water backup would weaken the pathway.

## C2050 — High-Load Convergence

**Drivers:** strategic materials, technical manufacturing, industrial
electrification, robotics, and generalized compute growth.

**Assumptions:** nuclear roles expand strategically but without license claims;
gas persists as transition/firming context; renewables and storage expand;
critical water/material loads and compute intensify.

**Persisting assets:** all named generation classes remain relevant reference
points except Monroe's planned retirement; no new reactor or local fusion plant
is assumed.

**Role changes:** Davis-Besse and Fermi 2 expand role; gas persists; Elmore,
Woodville, Collins Park, Bay View, and Oppidan expand roles; Monroe retires.

**New functions:** high-value grid modernization, critical-load resilience, and
industrial/compute convergence.

**Dependency changes:** electricity, cooling/water, grid coordination, storage,
and compute coupling increase qualitatively; external gas dependence persists.

**Constraints / failure modes:** permitting, cooling-water competition, fuel
security, supply chains, workforce, and concentration risk could block the
convergence. No precise load or deficit is calculated.

**Uncertainties / weakening evidence:** failure of industrial/compute investment
or lack of firm-power options would weaken the scenario substantially.

## C2075 — High-Load Convergence

**Drivers:** mature strategic manufacturing and compute intensity with uncertain
firm-generation choices.

**Assumptions:** nuclear outcomes are unknown rather than inevitable; gas remains
possible for firming; renewables and storage expand; water, materials, and
compute roles remain high-value and energy-intensive in qualitative terms.

**Persisting assets:** renewable, storage, water, materials, and compute
functions remain; Monroe is retired in the scenario; named nuclear assets remain
uncertain reference points.

**Role changes:** Elmore, Woodville, Collins Park, Bay View, and generalized
compute expand role; gas persists as a possibility without a capacity claim.

**New functions:** mature high-load coordination, critical-load resilience, and
industrial/compute convergence.

**Dependency changes:** high coupling among electricity, cooling/water, fuel,
weather, storage, communications, and strategic loads; concentration is a
potential concern, not a proven bottleneck.

**Constraints / failure modes:** cooling limits, external fuel exposure, supply
chain disruption, workforce, cyber/communications failure, and finite storage
could make the pathway unstable.

**Uncertainties / weakening evidence:** any evidence that strategic demand does
not materialize, or that firm-generation options are unavailable, weakens this
most speculative state.
