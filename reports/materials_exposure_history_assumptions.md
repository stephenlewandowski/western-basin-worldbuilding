# Phase 2C Materials / Exposure History Assumptions

## Evidence and chronology

- USACE's current Luckey summary is the primary chronology. NIOSH is
  corroborating evidence where its historical scope overlaps.
- Dates retain source precision. Year-only and month-only records are not
  converted to invented calendar days. `range` and `approximate` are explicit.
- The 1951–1952 scrap-storage date is approximate. USACE says the proposed
  magnesium processing did not occur and site investigations determined the
  scrap was not the cause of the site's radioactive contamination.
- AEC, contractor, GSA, DOE, and USACE roles change over time. Agency,
  operator, owner/control, and cleanup executor are not treated as synonyms.
- USACE future milestones (fieldwork through 2027, closeout 2027–2028, and a
  planned DOE Legacy Management transfer by 2030) are plans, not completed acts.

## Exposure and health

- A documented process plus an established general beryllium mechanism supports
  a `potential_exposure_pathway`; it does not establish that a particular worker
  was exposed or became ill.
- Soil/groundwater contamination records document environmental media, not a
  completed human exposure pathway.
- The seven pathway records intentionally contain zero documented individual
  exposure findings. Sensitization and chronic beryllium disease are contextual
  occupational hazards, not attributed Luckey or Elmore outcomes.
- Modern OSHA controls are regulatory context. Their inclusion is neither a
  compliance finding nor a claim that modern operations are risk-free.

## Spatial and operational uncertainty

- Map 09 uses facility points. No authoritative contamination-unit polygon was
  found suitable for repository integration, so no boundary, buffer, exposure
  radius, or worker-residence area is inferred.
- The 2025 USACE monitoring record supports groundwater impact at sampled
  onsite wells. It does not support an atlas-derived groundwater-flow direction,
  plume, surface-water connection, or downstream receptor claim.
- Remediation disposal is represented as a waste-logistics interface without a
  route or destination geometry.
- Elmore remains advanced strategic-material processing with nonlocal feed, not
  local extraction. Public sources do not justify treating it as operationally
  continuous with Luckey.
- Current facility process detail is generalized to publicly documented product
  and end-use categories. Customer lists, process quantities, and routes remain
  outside scope.
- The 2022 Materion–Kairos announcement is dated evidence of a relationship;
  its continuing 2026 operating status remains unresolved. The 2025 CFS
  relationship remains dated and source-qualified.
- The held Great Black Swamp candidate is not used. Indigenous history remains
  in its dedicated research layer because no directly sourced Phase 2C finding
  required its insertion here.

## Worldbuilding Implications

The factual history can support speculative themes of regional institutional
memory, remediation expertise becoming exportable capability, environmental
monitoring as industrial infrastructure, and persistent tension between
national strategic production and local trust. A future setting might treat old
cleanup sites as high-information observatories or make worker surveillance a
core civic technology. These are worldbuilding interpretations, not 2026 facts
and not Map 10 commitments.
