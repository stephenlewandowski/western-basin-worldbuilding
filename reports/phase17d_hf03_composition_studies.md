# Phase 17D HF-03 — Maumee Bio-Ag Landscape composition studies

**Art-direction record and human acceptance · 2026-09-28 · C / C-L1 accepted · S/K only**

The comparison and recommendation below preserve the pre-production composition experiment. The original statements that no final asset or visual acceptance existed describe that earlier stage; final human acceptance of C / C-L1 and the completed hero set is recorded at the end and in the [final production notes](phase17d_hf03_final_production_notes.md). Its sources are the [public visual guide](../docs/phase_briefs/phase17d_public_visual_style_guide.md), [Phase 17D synthesis](../docs/phase_briefs/phase17d_futurist_atlas_synthesis.md), [Glass Basin invention catalog](../docs/phase_briefs/phase17d_glass_basin_invention_catalog.md), [HF-03 concept record](phase17d_hero_futurescape_concepts.md), and [two-hero retrospective](phase17d_two_hero_visual_retrospective.md). HF-01 and HF-04 supply a standard of visible maintenance, functional translucency, ordinary work, and explanatory restraint; their brick, cranes, hanging modules, wet industrial pavement, amber sky, and camera placements are not a composition template.

## Common scene and the question being tested

All three options show **one synthetic Maumee drainage-landscape farm block** at one ordinary maintenance moment. The spatial reading must be **productive field → maintained bio-ag and machine-service seam → ditch and older drainage structure → wetland margin and downstream connection**. The field is still cultivated; the wetland is still a distinct ecological edge. Neither is a decorative foreground for a central futuristic building. The intervention is a **long folded working seam**: some sections are translucent cultivation or propagation envelope, others are opaque contained processing, service, qualification, or storage. Gaps and changes in finish make its history visible. It occupies a field margin rather than glazing the whole farm.

The common focal maintenance action is a **farmer inspecting and clearing a sedimented old drainage gate** at ground level. This is a visible decision point about access and water, not a claim that the gate improves downstream conditions. A **technician repairing a small field machine at an open docking berth**, with its wheel cover removed, provides a second, different form of work. A conventional delivery vehicle with purchased or otherwise external supplies is present in each frame, though not always prominent. A segregated hold point near an opaque processing module signals that a material or sample can await qualification or leave; no process result is depicted. An intentionally empty or isolated segment makes replacement possible without another suspended object.

Weather is **not** a shared condition for A, B, and C or a Glass Basin house palette. C remains the chosen composition; its next low-resolution round compares C-L1 bright broken cloud, C-L2 clear/hazy summer, and C-L3 cool diffuse overcast **without moving the camera, seam, people, gate, machinery, supply cue, or wetland edge**. Light must reveal the landscape relationships and functional materials rather than manufacture spectacle. The three treatments and first-round choice appear below.

The task for a small card is not to read every label. At **390 px**, a viewer should first see a farm landscape reorganized by a long maintained edge, then the ditch/wetland break, then one grounded work action. The later page caption or inset can explain qualification, external inputs, and authority. No option may imply a real mapped parcel, a Great Black Swamp restoration boundary, an engineered organism in the environment, measured yield, nutrient-removal success, ecological benefit, or a seamless circular farm.

## A — Field / ditch section dominant

**Proposition:** compose an almost sectional landscape photograph. The transect is the hero; the seam is an active middle layer rather than an object posed at the center. This is the clearest test of whether the viewer can follow productive and ecological spaces without fusing them into a single “green” outcome.

- **Camera height:** Slightly above an ordinary farm gate on a raised, muddy service bank. It is low enough to retain a farmer's scale and ditch depth, without becoming a surveyed aerial plan or an engineering cross-section.
- **Viewing direction:** Look diagonally *across* crop rows, through the folded working seam, over the drainage ditch, and toward reeds and a low wetland horizon. The sequence reads left-to-right and near-to-far even before annotation.
- **Foreground:** A worn gate, sediment against its lower edge, boot-marked clay, a field worker's hands, and one culvert rim. Keep the water control visually old and inspectable, not monumental or magically self-regulating.
- **Midground:** A limited run of propagation envelope, opaque processing/service bays, an empty isolated module position, a machine dock, and a narrow, muddy access lane. Alternate transparent, translucent, and opaque surfaces for specific functions.
- **Background:** Productive rows continue beyond the near field; a conventional supply vehicle appears near the older yard. The wetland margin and open drainage continuation remain visible past the ditch, without drawing a restoration boundary.
- **Human actions:** The farmer crouches to inspect or clear sediment at the gate. A technician works on the docked field machine at the seam. A driver may be present by the supply vehicle, but human figures should not form a theatrical lineup.
- **Machinery:** A small autonomous tool carrier is immobilized in an open berth, with a removed wheel cover and reachable service space. Opaque contained equipment remains fixed behind a separate service threshold; no machine floats among crops.
- **Cultivation:** Real crop rows occupy a substantial share of the frame. A few protected propagation bays are distinguishable from the unroofed productive field. Crop tissue is ordinary and weather-marked, with no miraculous uniform growth.
- **Drainage:** The old gate, ditch waterline, sediment marks, culvert, and bank give drainage a physical section. The farmer's task makes its maintenance legible; no arrow or color change suggests treated water downstream.
- **Wetland relationship:** Reeds and wetland cells lie beyond the ditch as a separate edge. A visible gap or service path distinguishes stewardship access from contained processing, and the frame does not show the wetland as a factory outlet.
- **External-input cue:** A conventional vehicle unloads supplies at the old yard or a visibly separate receiving threshold. Its presence establishes dependence on off-frame sources; no closed loop is drawn between ditch, crops, and modules.
- **Maintenance/failure cue:** Sediment obstructs the gate, the machine is open for repair, and one envelope or processing position is empty or isolated. These small interruptions must remain factual visual cues, not evidence of a particular failure rate.
- **Primary silhouette:** A shallow, stepped fold of pale envelope and opaque service strips divides crop rows from the ditch. The silhouette is continuous enough to reorganize the edge but visibly segmented and interrupted.
- **Light/weather:** Not fixed for this unselected composition. Any later test must separate clay, crop, envelope, ditch, and reeds; it should not inherit gray overcast or an amber storm break by default.
- **Material hierarchy:** Wet clay and living crop lead; weathered translucent panels and ribbed composite form the second layer; old timber or galvanized gate, culvert concrete, and sediment provide age. Contained media stays behind opaque walls.
- **At 390 px:** The field-to-seam-to-ditch bands and gate action should survive. The machine repair and segregated material logic may reduce to texture; they must be recoverable in a closer later view, not falsely “readable” at card size.
- **What remains K:** The folded edge, module arrangement, camera, crop type, worker actions, exact drainage interface, and the apparent relationship between processing and wetland are invented. No site or system efficacy is inferred.
- **Intentionally omitted:** A giant greenhouse, dramatic lift, detailed assay display, speculative organism close-up, aerial map, and distant industrial skyline. Do not use a wetland “after” image to imply restoration success.

**Strength and risk.** A is the best boundary explanation and would support a later diagram elegantly. Its risk is that the seam reads as a thin strip between two familiar landscapes, so the 2075 invention may disappear at thumbnail scale. If tested, give the folded edge enough depth and alternating function to change the farm section without letting it consume the field.

## B — Farm work dominant

**Proposition:** enter from the human maintenance threshold. The farmer's drainage task and the technician's machine repair occupy the foreground, with the bio-ag seam and field/wetland sequence still spatially legible around them. This option tests ordinary work most strongly, but it must not reduce the future form to a blurred backdrop.

- **Camera height:** Near a standing worker's eye level at the old gate and service lane, close enough for tools, mud, and hand positions to matter. Avoid a heroic low angle that turns routine repair into spectacle.
- **Viewing direction:** Look obliquely *along* the gate and ditch toward a machine berth, then across a break in the seam to visible crop rows. The wetland margin remains beyond the drainage line, not behind the processing bay.
- **Foreground:** The farmer's inspected gate, a hand tool or removable sediment screen, muddy boots, waterline marks, and a small sample of crop stubble or live rows. Ground textures establish that access is seasonal and laborious.
- **Midground:** The technician kneels or stands beside a stopped field machine in an open berth, its wheel cover set down safely. An adjacent translucent crop bay and opaque contained module show two different kinds of work.
- **Background:** Crop rows and older farmyard remain recognizable on one side; ditch continuation and reeds on the other. A conventional delivery vehicle enters the yard without being framed as a self-sufficient robotic supply network.
- **Human actions:** The farmer inspects or clears the sedimented gate before any adjustment. The technician checks the immobilized machine. A separate sample handler can transfer a sealed, labeled container at a small sheltered handoff, but the transfer does not certify the sample.
- **Machinery:** The docked autonomous field unit has visible tires, worn joints, service access, and a bounded turning path. Keep it small relative to people and fields. Contained processing stays housed rather than exposed as theatrical apparatus.
- **Cultivation:** Rows remain clearly productive and unroofed. A cultivation bay shows only a selective propagation function, with weathered translucent material and a service opening; it cannot stand in for an entirely engineered landscape.
- **Drainage:** The gate and ditch occupy a foreground line, with sediment and a culvert indicating an older system that still constrains the future intervention. Do not show water changing color or visibly “cleaning” at the structure.
- **Wetland relationship:** Reeds appear beyond the channel and an access margin, distinct from the field and processing zone. This separation matters even if the wetland is a narrow band in the frame.
- **External-input cue:** A driver handles purchased material at the yard or a receiving point; an outward track remains visible. No symbolic circular arrow, identical inbound/outbound container, or implied self-supply.
- **Maintenance/failure cue:** The machine is stopped with an open wheel housing, the gate is sedimented, and a sealed hold container is kept at the handoff. The scene can keep working while one unit is unavailable.
- **Primary silhouette:** A worker, small machine, and old gate make a grounded foreground triad; the folded seam continues behind them. It should read as an agricultural work edge, not a futuristic garage dropped into a field.
- **Light/weather:** Not fixed for this unselected composition. Light must keep faces, wet clay, exposed parts, crop leaves, and ditch legible without an amber rim light or backlit heroic pose.
- **Material hierarchy:** Muddy tires, crop tissue, sedimented timber/steel, and scuffed tool surfaces lead. Pale weathered envelope, ribbed composite, galvanized service metal, and contained opaque modules support the work.
- **At 390 px:** People and machine repair should read first; the gate and ditch should still be identifiable. If the seam collapses into background siding, B fails the house-style test despite succeeding as a human-interest image.
- **What remains K:** The particular farm, equipment, workers, gate configuration, handoff, and proximity of cultivation to processing are speculative arrangements. The scene cannot establish safe containment, material qualification, or drainage effect.
- **Intentionally omitted:** A hovering robot, human-free autonomous farm, polished greenhouse aisle, giant molecular laboratory prop, central crane or suspended cassette, and picturesque wetland “benefit” shot.

**Strength and risk.** B gives the clearest ordinary labor and the most credible close-up source for a future maintenance view. As the wide hero, it risks looking like a present-day farm repair photograph with new cladding in the background. It should only advance if the folded seam remains unmistakably a changed landscape section at small size.

## C — Bio-ag seam dominant

**Proposition:** make the long folded working edge the unmistakable 2075 silhouette while the field, old drainage control, and wetland remain separate, readable spaces. This is the most demanding balance: it must be a landscape invention, neither a central building nor a generic glass house.

- **Camera height:** Human height from a low field service track, slightly above the crop tops rather than above the whole farm. People, gate, and machine retain believable scale; the viewpoint never becomes a master-plan aerial.
- **Viewing direction:** Look obliquely *along* the seam so its folded translucent and opaque segments recede across the frame. Productive rows occupy the left/near side; the ditch traces the right side toward reeds and downstream space.
- **Foreground:** Crop rows break against wet clay and the older gate. The farmer at the drainage control anchors the near edge. A short, visibly empty seam bay or lifted-out ground-level panel sits within sight without becoming the focal “hanging object.”
- **Midground:** A long alternation of cultivation envelope, opaque contained processing, accessible service corridor, segregated hold storage, and an open machine berth gives the seam width and rhythm. The technician's stopped machine occupies one break in that rhythm.
- **Background:** The seam thins into older yard structures and a conventional delivery vehicle. Beyond the ditch, reeds and low wetland cells continue beneath open sky. Neither a port skyline nor a fantasy farm megastructure closes the horizon.
- **Human actions:** The farmer works at the sedimented gate; the technician repairs the small machine on blocks or at ground level. A driver or sample handler can mark the external-supply/qualification threshold, but the first two actions must be visible without labels.
- **Machinery:** A single small field machine is physically docked and open for repair. Other equipment, if present, is ordinary and partly obscured by service enclosures. Robotic action must not look like decorative movement through crops.
- **Cultivation:** Unroofed productive fields dominate one side. Selective propagation bays occupy only part of the seam; weathered membranes filter natural daylight over crops inside, while opaque bays remain visibly separate.
- **Drainage:** The old gate, culvert, ditch water, bank erosion, and sediment marks run beside the intervention. A gap allows maintenance access. The seam does not bridge the ditch as though it owns the downstream system.
- **Wetland relationship:** Reeds interrupt the engineered edge instead of being landscaped as its ornamental border. Show a clear service or ecological break; the wetland extends beyond the frame without a drawn restoration polygon.
- **External-input cue:** A conventional delivery vehicle and an inbound receiving threshold are visible near the yard; a separate outward service track remains plausible. Do not connect the vehicle, processing bay, field, and ditch into a glowing or color-coded closed circuit.
- **Maintenance/failure cue:** A near segment is empty or isolated, envelope generations differ in translucency and weathering, the machine is stopped, and the gate has sediment. These interruptions should add specificity without making the whole landscape a ruin.
- **Primary silhouette:** A **long folded, segmented edge** with alternating pale translucent ridges and lower opaque service volumes, ending against an old gate and ditch. It is a working seam, not a tall glass shed or a central pavilion.
- **Light/weather:** Compare C-L1, C-L2, and C-L3 below in this same frame. Each must model the folds and preserve the field-to-water sequence; none uses a golden horizon, deep concealment fog, or cinematic storm beam.
- **Material hierarchy:** Crops and wet clay establish the base; weathered membrane and ribbed composite define the new form; galvanized/oxidized service metal, worn tire rubber, old gate timber/steel, culvert concrete, sediment, reeds, and water show use and age.
- **At 390 px:** The folded seam, open field, and ditch/reed break must read as three distinct shapes. The farmer and machine berth should provide one visible scale/work cue. Tiny colored bins or unreadable signs are not substitutes for spatial separation.
- **What remains K:** The continuous seam, segmentation, specific modules, exact placement, field crop, workers, repair state, and downstream juxtaposition are invented. Contained biological work is a bounded optional interface, not a proven process.
- **Intentionally omitted:** A central brick building, a crane or suspended cassette, a farm-wide glass roof, floating laboratory equipment, decorative robots, mapped drainage routes, a glowing reactor-like module, and any picture of cleansed water or improved yields.

**Strength and risk.** C gives HF-03 the strongest single silhouette and most direct challenge to the established house style: can a maintained future feel related to HF-01/HF-04 while made of field, ditch, membrane, clay, and reeds? Its risk is becoming a broad shiny greenhouse whose service and ecological boundaries vanish. The low-resolution study must therefore show an opaque processing interval, a ground-level repair/empty bay, old drainage control, and real unroofed crops in the same frame.

## Comparison and first-study decision

| Test | A — transect | B — farm work | C — seam |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landscape sequence | Clearest field → seam → ditch → wetland reading. | Readable if the ditch does not monopolize the foreground. | Readable if the seam leaves enough field and water visible on both sides. |
| Human task | Farmer and technician are secondary but distinct. | Strongest worker-scale action. | Workers must be deliberately placed to remain visible beneath the large silhouette. |
| 2075 form at card scale | May flatten into a thin horizon line. | May retreat behind people and machine. | Strongest folded form and the hardest test of avoiding a generic greenhouse. |
| Main failure | Diagrammatic landscape with insufficient invention. | Present-day repair scene with future siding. | Shiny uninterrupted shed that swallows field and ditch. |

**Recommend C for exactly one low-resolution visual study.** This is a reviewable composition test, not permission to finish or publish an image. Frame the seam as a long segmented working edge and require the productive field, old gate, ditch, wetland margin, external supply, ground-level machine repair, and separated contained module to remain visible. The focal human act is the farmer inspecting and clearing the sedimented gate; the technician at the stopped field machine is a second, different task. If C fails at 390 px because it reads as a generic greenhouse or hides the ditch, return to A's transect layout before adding detail or finish. B can later inform a close maintenance view, but is weaker as the first hero-wide test.

**Review gate before any more production:** at a small card, identify the folded seam, productive field, and drainage/wetland break without reading labels; identify one work action and a separate service/failure condition at a larger site width; confirm that opaque containment and external input/output paths remain distinct. Reject a study that relies on explanatory prose to prove the basic landscape arrangement, that implies treatment or yield success, or that repeats a crane-lift poster. Preserve ample unroofed field and sky in the crop so the invention belongs to the Maumee landscape rather than an isolated building.

## C light/weather refinement — one composition, three treatments

These vary illumination and surface response only. The C camera, folded seam, productive field, gate, ditch, wetland break, two workers, stopped machine, external delivery, and empty service segment stay in place. None is a different season of infrastructure or a new A/B/C decision.

| Check | C-L1 — bright broken cloud | C-L2 — clear / hazy summer | C-L3 — cool diffuse overcast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sky | Late-summer/early-autumn broken cumulus over strong diffuse daylight; no storm front. | Pale blue with humidity haze or subtle heat shimmer. | Cool high cloud, with no dramatic opening. |
| Sun direction | Field-side cross-light through cloud breaks; no backlit sunburst. | Higher field-side sun; modest atmospheric haze. | Direct sun masked; broad sky fill. |
| Shadow character | Broad soft shadows with a few defined sun patches. | Harder folds and crop-row shadows. | Low-contrast, even shadows. |
| Crop color behavior | Varied green/gold without an idealized uniform crop. | Brighter, warmer leaves; preserve texture from clipping. | Cooler, muted crop tones. |
| Wet clay appearance | Recent-rain clay: dark ruts and selective highlights, not mirror paving. | Sunlit crust beside damp ruts and sediment. | Even dark moisture and visible boot/tire marks. |
| Translucent envelope | Natural light filters through weathered panels; opaque bays hold shape. | Stronger transmission; control glare and brochure gloss. | Surface wear reads, but folds need tonal separation. |
| Ditch / water readability | Sun patches distinguish waterline, bank, and sediment. | Control surface glare so water stays distinct from clay. | Separate water from bank despite subdued contrast. |
| Wetland edge readability | Reed texture alternates light/shade, without a “restored” glow. | Reed edge remains separate from bright crops and sky. | Avoid merging reeds with a gray horizon. |
| Machinery visibility | Side-light shows stopped chassis, open repair, and worn tires. | Hard shadows must not conceal the wheel repair. | Machine detail reads but may blend into service bay. |
| Human visibility | Farmer and technician remain lit as workers, not silhouettes. | Keep faces/tasks clear under stronger sun and hats. | Even light helps tasks; figures need color separation. |
| 390-pixel card behavior | Fold, open field, and ditch break read in alternating light. | Strong fold, but glare may bleach field/water distinctions. | Boundaries read quietly; silhouette risks flattening. |
| Primary risk | Cloud break becomes another dramatic storm or glossy farm advert. | Postcard pastoralism or generic “smart farm” perfection. | Repeats a default moody look and weakens form. |

**Recommend C-L1 for the first low-resolution HF-03 image study.** It offers more crop and material color than C-L3, and more controlled envelope and ditch legibility than C-L2. Broken cloud is ordinary agricultural daylight here, not a storm-break climax: keep the sun out of frame, avoid amber grading, and let patches of direct light clarify the folded seam and wet clay. C-L2 remains a useful robustness comparison; C-L3 retains the original boundary-reading comparison, not the house default. This is an illustration choice, not a scenario ranking or a final asset approval.

**Material/invention gate for C-L1:** the image must still show productive **open** field, long folded seam, old gate, distinct ditch and wetland break, small machine repair, conventional external supply, translucent cultivation beside opaque contained work, and one empty/isolated service segment. Brightness must not make the field idyllic, turn the panels into a greenhouse brochure, erase sediment and wear, or imply successful ecological restoration. Because no new image is rendered here, these are review criteria for the next study rather than claims that a finished image has passed them.

## Candidate invention test — no new catalog IDs

| Question | Adaptive crop / propagation envelope | Bio-ag edge processing module |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognizable silhouette? | **Provisionally yes** if weathered translucent folds alternate with opaque service strips and an empty segment. A continuous glass roof would erase it. | **Not alone yet.** An opaque box becomes recognizable only through a bounded service/qualification bay and a clear relation to the working seam. |
| Changes work and landscape section? | It can make propagation, unroofed field, and machine access occupy distinct but adjacent parts of the farm edge. | It can add contained handling, hold space, and outward movement without absorbing field cultivation or drainage. |
| Maintenance shapes form? | Replaceable envelope sections, access breaks, aged panels, and ground-level service define the folds. | Isolation clearance, reachable interfaces, spent media removal, and segregated storage must determine its volume; a sealed mystery box fails. |
| External inputs and outputs visible? | Purchased materials, energy/service access, and field deliveries remain outside the envelope. | A conventional receiving vehicle, hold/reject position, and outward residual/service path must survive the composition. Nothing automatically becomes useful stock. |
| Field separate from contained biology? | Selective bays occupy the seam while ordinary productive rows remain open. | Contained work stays behind opaque boundary and separate service access; the wetland is not its effluent display. |
| Visible, bounded failure? | One segment can be empty or mismatched while field work continues. | A held batch, isolated module, or removed media can interrupt operation without collapsing the farm or implying safe release. |
| More than a fancy greenhouse? | Only if envelope geometry makes service, access, and selective cultivation legible at landscape scale. | Only if qualification, containment, human acceptance, and outside dependence are spatial, not labels on a futuristic shed. |

**Catalog implication at the pre-production test stage:** the adaptive crop/propagation envelope was the stronger candidate for later development if study C passed its silhouette and service tests. The bio-ag edge processing module remained conditional until a later view could show containment, qualification, rejection, and external movement without implying nutrient performance or ecological benefit. The final human decision below supersedes this candidate status for the adaptive envelope; the processing module remains provisional.

## Claim boundary for human review

**E** is inherited Maumee agriculture, drainage, weather, field work, and ecological context; this document adds no measurement, map, or site reconstruction. **S** is conditional, maintained agricultural adaptation with external inputs, labor, qualification, and downstream responsibility. **K** is every proposed seam, crop envelope, contained module, machine arrangement, gate juxtaposition, human action, and visual relationship. The Great Black Swamp held geometry stays out of view. Molecular observation would support interpretation, not prove yield, nutrient removal, containment, wetland health, or safety. No environmental release, adoption rate, structural dimension, or successful circular exchange is asserted.

## Human acceptance decision — 2026-09-28

The final HF-03 hero wide, maintenance view, explanatory SVG, and public claims
all **PASS**. Composition **C — BIO-AG SEAM DOMINANT** and light/weather
**C-L1 — BRIGHT BROKEN CLOUD** are accepted visual choices; publication is
authorized. HF-03 extends the Glass Basin visual language beyond brick/steel
industrial Toledo. No new canon is created. **GBI-03 — Adaptive Crop /
Propagation Envelope** is established in the invention catalog; the Bio-Ag
Edge Processing Module remains provisional. These choices claim no yield,
nutrient-removal, ecological benefit, containment success, adoption, or
environmental outcome.
