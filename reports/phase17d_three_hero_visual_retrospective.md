## The durable grammar

**Transformed inheritance** is the most convincing common principle. Each image retains an older working system that continues to constrain the future: masonry, rail and public route in HF-01; gate, drainage and productive land in HF-03; hall, crane and freight in HF-04. The old system is neither a picturesque ruin nor proof that any depicted retrofit could be built. It gives the invented future an obligation to connect, be accessed, wear and be repaired. HF-03 is decisive evidence that the rule does not require brick. Its wet clay and drainage control carry as much temporal weight as the older industrial shells.

**Technology has thickness.** HF-01's leaves require mounting and a walkable service rib; HF-03's crop envelope is selective and interrupted by opaque contained/service work; HF-04's exchange requires a protected gallery, a berth and separated dispositions. Glass is functional where it filters daylight, protects inspection or allows a bounded view. Opacity is equally functional where equipment, isolation, storage or privacy need it. A repeated shiny material is not the house style. The stronger visual test is whether changing or removing the new system would alter routes, section, labor and boundaries.

**The future can stop.** All three sets show a material interruption, but in different ways. HF-01's mismatched leaf and exposed replacement make repair visible; HF-04's empty berth and held batch refuse automatic exchange; HF-03's blocked gate and stopped field machine make routine inspection the actual story. The latter is a useful correction to the first pair. Repair need not be an oversized mid-air event. The future also remains open to conventional delivery, freight, power and human decisions. Incomplete systems, isolation and outside dependencies make the images more specific, not less futuristic.

**People have tasks, while authority has a boundary.** A glazier guiding a leaf, a farmer clearing sediment and a quality worker holding a batch are visually different forms of judgment. The images cannot show the whole property agreement, qualification protocol, water responsibility or operating authorization at thumbnail scale. Their pages and insets must distinguish visible action from institutional decision. An orange vest, security fence or data label alone does not establish governance. Human scale is useful when it changes the viewer's understanding of access, timing or stoppage; it is insufficient as decoration.

**Weather reveals use.** HF-03's clear broken-cloud daylight is a successful departure from the two industrial images. It allows crops, envelope, muddy track and reeds to remain separate and gives the third hero a different public energy. HF-01 uses its storm opening to illuminate the canopy; HF-04 uses dusty hall light to explain volume. None licenses a default storm, haze or golden rim light. For a Lake Erie edge, wind, visibility, glare, fouling and seasonal access should determine the atmosphere.

## Family resemblance and its risks

The published sets have a useful family resemblance in serviceable joints, selective transparency, worn material, ordinary people, mixed old/new fabric and an E/S/K caption. They also share an editorial rhythm: arresting wide image, distinct close work view, then plain qualitative inset. That rhythm is now strong enough to carry a fourth hero with a different camera and weather.

The most dangerous repetition is **a replacement object staged for spectacle**. HF-01 and HF-04 both feature a suspended piece, old brick, a high-visibility crew, wet reflective ground and a bright exterior beyond a darker work zone. Their engineering propositions differ, but the framing can still teach the wrong house rule: “hang a module in an old industrial place.” HF-03 avoids the hanging object and brick, but its long clean service mass can slip toward a generic greenhouse/logistics campus. At card scale the old gate and reeds establish place; the opaque contained function and segregated decisions need the maintenance image and inset. This is a limit of the wide image, not permission to label it as a demonstrated agricultural process.

HF-01's port skyline is a cue, not a unique Toledo proof. HF-04's crane hall could be any deindustrialized factory without its freight, quality and material logic. HF-03's crop rows and greenhouse-like bays could be an agritech advertisement without the old drainage work, wet clay, open field and visibly separate wetland. The next image should therefore gain distinctiveness from **Lake Erie exposure and recovery operations**, not from adding a fourth skyline, more glass or another crane.

At 390 px the three home cards remain differentiated, but not every actor survives. HF-01's leaf sequence and lifted piece are immediate; HF-04's tall rack and suspended cell survive against the dark hall; HF-03 reads first as field, long intervention and water boundary, with the farmer and machine small. This supports a strict card test for HF-02: horizontal terrace, open lake and one crew/recovery cue must survive without depending on text, colored tags, fine screen divisions or a speculative energy label. At banner scale it must still allow readers to follow land → protected service → quay → lake.

## Invention-catalog review after three heroes

The [catalog](../docs/phase_briefs/phase17d_glass_basin_invention_catalog.md) needs **no edit in this transaction**. GBI-01 already defines the optical civic canopy by directional leaves, replacement edges, attachment and service/public route; its brick street is contextual. GBI-02 already defines a qualified modular cell by isolated service, protected checking, segregated outcomes and outside freight; the crane is one possible means. GBI-03 is now established from accepted HF-03, with selective propagation, an interrupted field-edge envelope and visible external inputs. Its description correctly excludes farm-wide enclosure and performance claims. The observed card-scale greenhouse risk is a reason to enforce its service and open-field tests, not to rename it or add a claim.

The Bio-Ag Edge Processing Module remains provisional: adjacency to a field is not enough to specify containment, qualification or an outward path. The corrected HF-02 low-resolution study has now tested the **Lake instrument recovery cradle** and **Weather-screened inspection gallery** strongly enough to continue both into final production. They remain unnumbered K candidates, not GBI IDs. Promotion waits until the hero wide, maintenance/detail and explanatory inset pass human review; GBI-01/02/03 remain unchanged.

## Consequence for final HF-02 production planning

HF-02 is the next approved **final-production experiment**, not a ranking of futures. Human review accepted **B — Service Terrace / Coast Section Dominant** and **L1 — Clear, Windy Lake Day** for final-production planning after the corrected low-resolution study. Preserve the accepted civilian environmental-survey / observation-craft reading, a dry cradle that reads as a maintenance interface, reachable-height weather-screen replacement, instrument comparison/calibration, ordinary utility/quay work, and human responsibility for recovery, servicing, calibration and access. Show “security” through accountable access, not militarized imagery. Keep the real Toledo intake crib a separate identifiable reference asset; do not attach the speculative terrace to it.

The corrected low-resolution test passed strongly enough to proceed; no further composition exploration is requested before final-production planning. Final HF-02 production has **not begun**: the final hero is not accepted, no final assets exist, and HF-02 is not published. The [HF-02 composition study](phase17d_hf02_composition_studies.md) records the accepted direction and optional ecological/seasonal cues. No new GBI ID or C is created, HF-05 remains future work, and the public site must remain unchanged until the final HF-02 set passes human review.
