# Freight Evidence and Interchange Findings

## Port of Toledo

Phase 5B strengthens the Port of Toledo from a broad gateway to a better-
documented set of access and movement interfaces. Port sources support 13
terminals/terminal operators, Facility #1 general cargo warehouse, adjacent
liquid-bulk transloading, on-dock Class I rail access, I-75 and I-80/90 access,
Michigan/Canada heavy-haul context, and Ironville vessel input with truck/rail
output.

These findings remain port-level. They do not assign every terminal to every
carrier, identify a customer, or create an exact dock-to-road/rail route.

## Class I and short-line interchange

NTAD geometry confirms the public Class I corridor context for Norfolk Southern,
CSX, and Canadian National in the bounded study area. The Port of Toledo source
strengthens their relevance as public port/corridor interfaces. Wheeling & Lake
Erie is publicly listed by the port but remains unresolved as a specific
interchange because a public source naming a particular port movement or line
use was not established.

The phase does not map dispatch infrastructure, yards, sidings, hazardous
routing, or security arrangements.

## Modal interchange

Marine↔rail and marine↔highway access are documented at the Port of Toledo level.
The Ironville source documents vessel input and truck/rail finished-product
outputs, but not the physical transfer arrangement. Rail↔highway interchange
therefore remains a documented multimodal relationship rather than a detailed
facility layout.

The evidence crosswalk distinguishes physical access, documented interchange,
documented shipment relationship, and corridor-only context.

## Facility-to-corridor evidence

- Port of Toledo: documented facility access and documented modal interfaces.
- Ironville: documented vessel input and truck/rail output; destination remains
  generalized.
- Area Aggregates Woodville: facility/product identity, but freight access is
  proximity-only.
- Martin Marietta Woodville: processing identity, but freight access is
  proximity-only.
- Graymont Genoa: processing identity; quarry/feed and freight access remain
  unresolved.
- Materion Elmore: advanced processing and nonlocal feed remain documented, but
  freight mode and route remain generalized.
- Luckey: licensed off-site remediation disposal is a documented movement
  function without route geometry.

## Commodity evidence

Strongest evidence supports:

- general cargo and industrial materials;
- bulk and liquid-bulk port functions;
- steel/metal and HBI-related Ironville movement;
- Class I rail and heavy-haul interfaces; and
- generalized aggregate, lime, strategic-material, and remediation logistics.

FAF supports broad agricultural/bulk commodity context, but no Toledo-specific
grain shipment or elevator relationship is upgraded.

## Materials Corridor decision

**B — FREIGHT EVIDENCE PROVIDES WEAK SUPPORT.**

Phase 5B provides the strongest factual support so far for a Woodville/Genoa/
Elmore/Luckey/Toledo industrial-material network. It still does not establish a
single coherent corridor, continuous facility-to-facility movement chain, named
route, common operator, polygon, jurisdiction, or sixth macroregion.

The recommended treatment is to retain “Materials Corridor” as a loose network
and worldbuilding motif while refusing to treat it as formal geography.

## Remaining evidence gaps

- Port-specific agricultural/grain movement evidence remains incomplete.
- W&LE interchange and line-use evidence remains insufficient.
- Facility-level access for Woodville and Genoa remains unresolved.
- Elmore transport mode and customer relationships remain unverified.
- Modal transfer layouts, schedules, quantities, and destination facilities remain
  outside scope.
