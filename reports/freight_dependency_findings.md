# Freight Dependency Findings

## FACTUAL / ANALYTICAL FINDINGS

## Port / Marine Gateway

The Port of Toledo is the clearest concentration point in the factual freight
layer. Public evidence supports marine access, Great Lakes/St. Lawrence external
orientation, on-dock Class I rail, major highway access, heavy-haul connection,
general cargo, liquid-bulk transloading, and the Ironville vessel/truck/rail
interface. The port is therefore a high qualitative gateway dependency, but no
volume or universal terminal dependence is asserted.

## Rail

NS, CSX, and CN provide public Class I corridor context. Rail is a moderate
regional dependency in the absence of facility-specific use evidence. W&LE is a
publicly listed port interface but its specific interchange and substitutability
remain unknown.

## Highway

I-75 and I-80/90 have documented Port of Toledo access. I-280 and US-23 are
regional freight corridor context. Highway dependence is high for broad general
industry and moderate for selected materials functions, but no plant-specific
truck route is assigned.

## Generalized Fuel / Pipeline Interfaces

The external fuel interface is high in external orientation but low in evidence
confidence and modal substitutability. EIA supports general petroleum/fuel
context; no pipeline alignment, capacity, contract, or facility dependence is
modeled.

## Major Material / Industrial Nodes

Elmore is strongly externally oriented because nonlocal feed and strategic
end-use context are documented, but its freight mode and route remain unknown.
Woodville and Genoa have sourced material-processing roles but no verified
facility-to-corridor access in this phase. Luckey has a documented licensed
off-site remediation-disposal interface without route geometry.

## Agricultural / Bulk System

Agricultural/bulk external orientation is supported at broad FAF context level.
Toledo-specific grain, elevator, and port dependence remain unknown. Bulk
marine and agricultural substitutions are not treated as operationally proven.

## External Market Connections

The region is oriented toward Great Lakes/St. Lawrence, Midwest industrial,
Michigan/Canada, and licensed remediation-disposal interfaces. These are broad
market/destination classes, not named commercial routes.

## Cross-System Information Dependencies

The primary concentrations are:

- Port of Toledo as a multi-modal gateway;
- external-market orientation for Ironville and Elmore;
- highway dependence for broad general industry and port access;
- rail dependence where port and Ironville evidence is strongest; and
- external fuel dependence with low public detail.

No concentration is translated into a security target or failure prediction.

## Modal Substitutability

General industrial goods have moderate broad modal flexibility. Bulk marine,
strategic materials, and external fuel have low or unknown substitutability in
this evidence set. The result reflects missing operational equivalence evidence,
not a claim that substitution is impossible.

## WORLDBUILDING IMPLICATIONS

The factual layer supports later themes of gateway dependence, port-city
relationships, multimodal industrial geography, logistics institutional power,
external-market orientation, and tension between local production and external
supply. These are interpretive possibilities, not current vulnerability claims.

## Boundaries and remaining gaps

Phase 5C does not establish shipment volumes, exact routes, hazardous-material
movement, pipeline capacity, or modal failure consequences. Port-specific grain
evidence, W&LE interchange, Woodville/Genoa access, and Elmore transport mode
remain the highest-value evidence gaps.
