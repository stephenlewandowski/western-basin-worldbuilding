## Purpose and product

Translate the lived systems of the 2075 Western Basin into human points of view that expose maintenance, dependence, institutional friction, ordinary life, uncertainty, and conflicting obligations without turning characters into exposition devices.

Construction runs PLACE → SYSTEM → ROLE → ROUTINE → DEPENDENCY → PRESSURE → CHOICE → CONSEQUENCE, never character concept → a world invented to accommodate it. The world must remain intelligible without these people.

Produce one [role-and-story seed matrix](../../reports/phase17c_role_and_story_seed_matrix.md): ten unnamed roles, a small typed relationship map, and six bounded narrative-thread seeds. This brief and the seeds form one production transaction, not a planning-only stage. No novel outline, screenplay, game dialogue, scenes, full biographies, named-character canon, final world canon, or new scientific modeling.

Epistemic boundaries govern what is true in the world, not what every character believes or chooses. Characters may misunderstand evidence, prefer speed to completeness, protect income, status, time or family obligations, overestimate a technology, distrust a correct observation, defer an awkward problem, or choose a defensible option that later proves costly. Such beliefs and choices remain K: the narrative must not convert a mistaken or interested inference into an accepted scientific/world claim, or make characters deliberately irrational merely to create conflict.

## 1. Character construction

Each viable role carries place; social/institutional role; routine; technical competence or practical knowledge; something depended on; something depending on them; authority possessed and explicitly not possessed; recurring maintenance burden; personal stake; institutional obligation; information they cannot fully know; a choice among genuine alternatives under pressure, rather than an automatic selection of the methodologically ideal response; and consequences propagating beyond them. Not every character is a technical expert. Competence can mean knowing a household schedule, a loading routine, or how to get a notice corrected. Let personal stakes resist the neat institutional answer rather than merely illustrate it.

## 2. Narrative scale

Prefer shifts, inspections, repair windows, sampling routes, public notices, maintenance delays, access disputes, procurement problems, seasonal transitions, institutional handoffs, conflicting observations, household/work schedules, freight interruptions, weather constraints, and responsibility disputes. Large systemic consequences can emerge from ordinary work; a thread need not resolve the system it reveals.

Do not default to apocalypse, total collapse, world-saving missions, secret supertechnology, a singular evil corporation, omniscient AI, or chosen-one structures. At least three threads must remain interesting when technology works normally: timing, ownership, labor, access, liability, weather, priorities, and incomplete jurisdiction can supply conflict.

## 3. Narrative causality

Preserve these boundaries in every consequence branch:

- dependency != guaranteed failure
- propagation != probability
- observation != control
- AI recommendation != authority
- response available != successful
- adaptation != elimination
- technology != automatic protection
- detection != population burden
- surveillance signal != incidence
- presence != transmission
- infection != clinical disease
- contamination != exposure != dose != illness

Consequence paths are alternatives, not forecasts or ranked outcomes. Residual != recoverable resource != qualified input; more connected != self-sufficient. A fictional authority allocation is a K option, not a claim about future law.

## 4. Character diversity

Vary profession, institutional position, age/life stage, technical expertise, relationship to infrastructure, economic security, public/private responsibility, institutional trust, reliance on automation, and access to maintenance resources. Use provisional life-stage contrasts without exact ages or biographies. Do not fill identity quotas or give demographic attributes moral or ideological meaning.

Include at least three ordinary-life/non-controller perspectives; someone with very little formal authority; substantial technical competence without corresponding legal authority; and formal authority with incomplete technical knowledge. Roles should not all know one another or belong to one institution.

## 5. Place specificity

Use Glass City Core, Maumee River Commons, Great Lakes Industrial Belt, Lake Erie Energy & Security Coast, and Black Swamp Country as overlapping interpretive macroregions, not service territories or jurisdictions. Black Swamp Country is interpretive only; no held Great Black Swamp geometry.

Work through lake weather, river/drainage landscapes, working waterfronts, old industrial fabric, fields/ditches/wetlands, rail/freight/service yards, retrofitted buildings, and mixed old/new technology. Do not invent exact future facilities, parcels, routes, or deployment maps.
