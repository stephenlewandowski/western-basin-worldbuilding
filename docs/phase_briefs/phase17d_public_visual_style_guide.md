# Phase 17D — public visual style guide

**Working art direction · 2075 futurescapes · S/K development material · no new canon**

The public Atlas should lead with an arresting, inhabited future place. Each opening image must show how technology changes the *section and use* of that place; a maintenance view and one explanatory inset then make the proposition legible. Existing Phase 17A protocol sheets remain supporting graphics and evidence paths, not the headline image. This guide governs presentation, not engineering feasibility or a final setting design.

## House rules

- **Recognizable place, transformed form.** Retain Western Basin material, work, weather, and infrastructure cues. Give each future a distinct silhouette: HF-01 is a broad optical street canopy; HF-04 is a tall replaceable industrial interior. Use composite views, not invented surveyed parcels or named-facility retrofits.
- **Convergence must occupy space.** Optics, robotics, compute, energy, freight, and human authority need visible interfaces, service volumes, and boundaries. If a device can be removed without changing the architecture or daily use, it is scenery.
- **Make repair part of the spectacle.** Show replaceable bays, lifting and access routes, isolation points, worn surfaces, missing or mismatched pieces, and a worker with a credible task. Keep the repair readable at landing-page scale.
- **Atmosphere carries location.** Great Lakes humidity, rain, river light, winter glare, dust, and industrial shade should shape depth, surfaces, and people’s movement. Use a deliberate light hierarchy and a strong focal silhouette; avoid uniform gray or decorative fog that hides the system.
- **Manufactured, aged materials.** Distinguish patterned/translucent glass, protective glazing, old masonry and concrete, steel, seals, timber, and patched ground. Light should pass through or be filtered by functional glass; it should not turn every surface into mirror silver or generic blue glow.
- **Ordinary occupation.** Public routes, waiting, deliveries, quality checks, cleaning, and supervised maintenance show who can use a space and who decides. People should be legible as users and workers, without inventing named characters or implying universal access.
- **Editorial restraint.** Let the hero read without callout clutter; use the inset for labels. Keep clear alt text and a visible **composite 2075 scenario / K architectural form** caption. Distinguish inherited E context, conditional S relationships, and K form in the page copy. No quantitative geometry, performance, deployment, or benefit claims without a source.

## Avoid

Generic cyberpunk neon; spotless glass utopias; a familiar 2026 scene with attached gadgets; seamless automation or zero-waste loops; implied surveyed sightlines; anonymous “future city” skylines; protocol diagrams scaled up to impersonate a hero; and photorealistic polish that makes a speculative assembly look approved for construction.
