# Seasons, heat and ordinary adaptation

**Living art-direction amendment · 5 October 2026 · future work only**

Weather is a system condition, not a palette. Start with the
[observed climate and scenario boundaries](../reports/climate_integration_2026-10-05.md),
then choose a plausible fictional day. Existing accepted artwork remains unchanged;
its [visual audit](../reports/climate_visual_audit_2026-10-05.md) supplies no inferred
temperatures. This guide changes neither scientific freezes nor canon.

## Compact check for every future public illustration

- Name **season, time of day and thermal condition** before selecting lighting.
  State whether a number/condition is observed, a sourced scenario envelope or a
  fictional scene assignment. Never invent a single authoritative 2075 temperature.
- Identify **sun and shade**, occupied indoor/outdoor space, wind/humidity context
  and relevant warm-night recovery. A glazed roof can admit heat as well as light.
- Explain **clothing/PPE** through the task. Lightweight clothes, short sleeves
  and rolled sleeves fit some work; lifting, welding, chemicals, biological work
  and fall hazards can require protection. Show shade, breaks and hydration
  instead of universally removing protective clothing.
- Show **one ordinary response**: move a stool, fill a bottle, delay a repair,
  queue in shade, work early, change a filter or seek a cooler room. Leave space
  for pleasure, leisure and mild annoyance; adaptation is not perpetual emergency.
- Make **cooling/refuge and heat rejection** spatial where relevant: conditioned
  occupied room, hot process compartment, shaded access and reachable service.
  Give condensate/water a plausible path; no performance claims without evidence.
- Decide **air-quality context** separately from temperature: haze, smoke, ozone
  and cloud are different. Haze is not an ozone measurement. Ventilation and
  filtration can compete with cooling needs; avoid a wall of dashboards.
- Where the lake matters, distinguish **location, surface/depth, wind/mixing and
  ice season**. A surface average, low-ice winter or water color cannot establish
  hypoxia, blooms or intake safety.
- Check the condition against **place continuity**. Keep the Hall counter
  front-left, public reading floor open, instrument spine right, field/custody
  threshold left-rear and staff/service behind rooms. Compute/preservation remains
  beyond the tall right end. Shade or plant access must fit those relationships.

Record these answers in the existing scene/production brief, not a new approval
workflow. Suggested fields: `season`, `time_of_day`, `thermal_condition`,
`scenario_source`, `solar_exposure`, `wind_humidity_context`,
`clothing_ppe_rationale`, `cooling_or_refuge`, `air_quality_context`,
`lake_ice_context`, `ordinary_adaptation_action`, `invention_limits`.

## A broader seasonal vocabulary

**Hard midsummer sun:** bright pavement outside deliberate shade; accessible water
and cooling; worn reflective roofs; a canopy managing solar gain; lightweight work
protection where permitted. Heat shimmer is secondary to a human action.

**Warm night:** spaces still occupied after dark, a late maintenance shift, someone
seeking a cooler floor or interrupted sleep. Closed windows may express poor
outdoor air; do not promise a cool night after every hot day.

**Wet growing season / dry interval:** keep muddy gate repair and productive green
fields in the portfolio. Add constrained irrigation, soil-water conservation or
early work as separate scene conditions; extreme rain and drought can occur in
different intervals without making them one simultaneous event.

**Variable winter coast:** portray a specified ice/wind context. Low average ice
does not eliminate high-ice years. Open water alone cannot prove warming.

**Summer lake haze:** keep sky/water visibility distinct from pollution readings;
use measurement work and depth-aware props to communicate uncertainty.

Priority: one summer Glass City shade interruption, followed by a contrasting
warm-night scene. Both should serve a person's immediate want, not illustrate a
climate-policy lecture. No existing image is regenerated by this pass.
