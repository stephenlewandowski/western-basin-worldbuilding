# Glasspunk / Cyberglass Worldbuilding References

**Purpose:** Standalone reference and study guide for developing the Glasspunk / Cyberglass setting as a science-informed, real-place speculative world based initially on Toledo, Northwest Ohio, the Maumee watershed, and western Lake Erie.

**Use:** This file focuses on worldbuilding theory, craft, practical methods, visual/cartographic development, and resources that can inform novels, short stories, games, atlases, simulations, and related media.

**Working principle:** Build a coherent world of consequences, but reveal only the parts needed by characters and stories.

---

## 1. Core Worldbuilding Theory and Method

### J.R.R. Tolkien — *On Fairy-Stories*

**Type:** Foundational theory of fantasy and sub-creation  
**Priority:** High for theory; not a procedural manual  
**Full access:** Usually requires a book/library copy  
**Useful online resource:** Tolkien Estate scholarly guide  
https://www.tolkienestate.com/scholarship/verlyn-flieger-on-fairy-stories/

#### Core ideas

Tolkien's important contribution to worldbuilding is the idea of **sub-creation**: the author constructs a Secondary World with enough internal coherence that readers can enter it on its own terms. Credibility does not come primarily from realism in the narrow sense. It comes from the **inner consistency of reality**.

A strange premise becomes convincing when its consequences are coherent. A speculative element should therefore not be decorative; it should alter other parts of the world.

Tolkien also discusses:

- Secondary Worlds
- sub-creation
- internal consistency
- recovery: seeing familiar things afresh
- escape as a legitimate imaginative function
- consolation and eucatastrophe

#### Glasspunk takeaways

- Treat every important innovation as causal.
- If fusion becomes viable, energy markets, infrastructure, labor, compute, security, and politics change.
- If portions of the Great Black Swamp are restored, agriculture, hydrology, mosquitoes, birds, development pressure, and land politics change.
- Use speculative Toledo to create **recovery**: make readers see Lake Erie, glass manufacturing, mayflies, farms, river infrastructure, and neighborhoods as if newly encountered.
- Do **not** imitate Tolkien by believing a complete mythology, language family, and ancient chronology are prerequisites for writing.

### Mark J. P. Wolf — *Building Imaginary Worlds: The Theory and History of Subcreation*

**Type:** Academic theory of worldbuilding  
**Priority:** High for world architecture and canon  
**Publisher:** Routledge  
**Access:** Library/ebook recommended; previews and contents available online  
https://www.routledge.com/Building-Imaginary-Worlds-The-Theory-and-History-of-Subcreation/Wolf/p/book/9780415631198

#### Core ideas

Wolf distinguishes **worldbuilding from storytelling**. A story occurs in a world, but the world can extend beyond any individual narrative and across media.

Especially useful concepts include:

- **Invention:** what differs from the Primary World?
- **Completeness:** how strongly does the world imply existence beyond what is shown?
- **Consistency:** do facts and consequences remain compatible?
- transnarrative worlds
- transmedia expansion
- maps and timelines as world infrastructure
- canon and competing levels of authority
- retcons and continuity management

#### Glasspunk takeaways

Glasspunk can be conceived as a world first and a collection of outputs second:

**world model → stories → maps → atlas → art → simulations → tabletop/digital games**

Maintain distinctions between:

- canonical fact
- disputed historical interpretation
- faction belief
- rumor
- propaganda
- experimental/noncanonical scenario

The world may be much larger than any one Glasspunk story.

### Trent Hergenrader — *Collaborative Worldbuilding for Writers and Gamers*

**Type:** Practical worldbuilding framework  
**Priority:** Very high; one of the most directly useful references  
**Publisher:** Bloomsbury Academic  
**Access:** Book recommended, but substantial free worksheets/resources exist  
Official resources:  
https://www.collaborativeworldbuilding.com/

#### Core ideas

Hergenrader provides a structured process that begins with broad constraints before filling the world with details.

Important framework elements include:

1. audience and genre
2. scope
3. sequence/time
4. perspective
5. geographic boundaries and focal points
6. governing world premise/metanarrative
7. social structures
8. people, places, groups, events, and objects

Social structures are organized into four broad groups:

- **Governance**
- **Economics**
- **Social Relations**
- **Cultural Influences**

The method emphasizes relationships and trends rather than static cultural descriptions.

#### Glasspunk takeaways

This can serve as the main **development skeleton** for the setting.

For Toledo 2050–2080, define:

**Governance:** municipal, state, federal, Great Lakes regional, Indigenous, corporate, cooperative.

**Economics:** water, agriculture, compute, glass/materials, logistics, restoration, biotechnology, energy.

**Social relations:** labor, migration, education, urban/rural identity, wealth, data access.

**Cultural influences:** sports, neighborhoods, Great Lakes identity, industrial memory, restoration culture, Indigenous continuity, technical/apprenticeship culture.

Only then create factions, institutions, and characters from these forces.

### Trent Hergenrader & Stephen Slota — *The Worldbuilding Workshop: Teaching Critical Thinking and Empathy Through World Modeling, Simulation, and Play*

**Type:** Systems modeling, scenario building, simulation, role-play  
**Priority:** Very high for Glasspunk's science and scenario approach  
**Publisher:** MIT Press, 2025  
**Access:** Full book via purchase/library; excellent free companion resources  
Publisher:  
https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262553339/the-worldbuilding-workshop/

Official project/resources:  
https://www.theworldbuildingworkshop.com/  
https://www.theworldbuildingworkshop.com/resources

#### Core ideas

The method treats worldbuilding as a form of **world modeling** rather than merely fictional decoration.

Its six-stage process combines:

- hypothesis formation
- research
- discussion
- modeling
- analysis of governmental/economic/social/cultural systems
- geography and demography
- character construction
- simulation and role-play

The free resources include a **Collaborative Worldbuilding Card Deck** and a **Worldbuilding Structures Worksheet**.

#### Glasspunk takeaways

This is particularly appropriate because Glasspunk begins with a real-world baseline.

A possible workflow:

**Toledo / western Lake Erie 2026 baseline**  
↓  
change assumptions and drivers  
↓  
**2035 transition**  
↓  
**2050 emergence**  
↓  
**2075 mature Glasspunk region**  
↓  
place characters inside the resulting institutions and conflicts

Use simulations and structured role-play to test competing perspectives during events such as:

- water contamination
- nutrient-loading disputes
- regional blackouts
- compute curtailment
- infectious disease outbreaks
- engineered-organism escape
- major flood/derecho
- Great Lakes migration pressure
- cyberattack on water or grid infrastructure

## 2. Culture, Anthropology, and Lived Worlds

### Ursula K. Le Guin — *Always Coming Home*

**Type:** Fictional ethnography / experimental future-world presentation  
**Priority:** Very high; perhaps the closest conceptual precedent for a Glasspunk atlas  
**Access:** Full book via library/purchase; important visual supplements online  
Official maps and drawings:  
https://www.ursulakleguin.com/maps-and-drawings-index

#### Core ideas

*Always Coming Home* constructs a far-future society in what is now California using an ethnographic form rather than a conventional single plot.

The work mixes:

- fiction
- oral-history-like accounts
- poetry
- songs
- maps
- drawings
- rituals
- cultural commentary
- ordinary objects and practices

Some maps do more than show objective geography; they reveal how the fictional culture itself conceptualizes space.

#### Glasspunk takeaways

Glasspunk maps should not all come from an omniscient narrator.

Possible competing maps:

- Great Lakes Climate Authority watershed map
- farm cooperative nutrient map
- Indigenous stewardship map
- power-grid operator map
- Coast Guard security map
- insurance-risk map
- migration-bird route map
- teenager/transit subculture map
- property-development promotional map
- neighborhood exposure-justice map

Create world authenticity through artifacts:

- transit passes
- apprenticeship credentials
- sports programs
- food menus
- repair manuals
- water alerts
- environmental dashboards
- field notebooks
- election literature
- advertisements
- school notices
- seed catalogs
- ecological monitoring forms

---

### Ursula K. Le Guin — “The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction”

**Type:** Narrative theory / cultural critique  
**Priority:** High and short  
**Access:** Official overview and publication information online; essay appears in *Dancing at the Edge of the World* and standalone editions  
https://www.ursulakleguin.com/the-carrier-bag-theory-of-fiction

#### Core ideas

Le Guin challenges the idea that civilization and narrative must be organized around heroic conquest, weapons, and linear victory.

The **container**—bag, basket, bottle, home, storage vessel—becomes an alternative model of technology and storytelling.

This encourages stories built around:

- gathering
- maintaining
- repairing
- carrying
- preserving
- nurturing
- coordinating

rather than only conquering.

#### Glasspunk takeaways

Strong protagonists need not be warriors or CEOs.

Consider:

- water-treatment operators
- environmental technicians
- wetland crews
- grid dispatchers
- nurses
- seed keepers
- mechanics
- insect surveyors
- exposure scientists
- librarians/archivists
- parents
- civil engineers
- food-system workers

These occupations are ideal for a solarpunk/cyberpunk world because they reveal civilization through maintenance.

---

### Ursula K. Le Guin — *Steering the Craft*

**Type:** Fiction-writing craft  
**Priority:** High once actual stories begin  
**Access:** Full book via library/purchase; official page provides information and excerpts  
https://www.ursulakleguin.com/steering-the-craft

#### Core ideas

Focuses on the actual mechanics of prose:

- sentence rhythm
- point of view
- narrative voice
- syntax
- repetition
- narrative distance
- exercises and revision

#### Glasspunk takeaways

The world model can be highly technical; the prose usually should not be.

A nutrient model may track dozens of variables.

A story may only need:

> The Maumee smelled wrong that morning.

The model supplies causal depth. The prose supplies lived experience.

## 3. Worldbuilding Checklists and Causal Audits

### Patricia C. Wrede — “Fantasy Worldbuilding Questions”

**Type:** Extensive diagnostic checklist  
**Priority:** Very high; free and directly usable  
**Access:** Legally available online through SFWA and Wrede's site  
Main SFWA page:  
https://sfwa.org/2009/08/04/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions/

Author site:  
https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions/

#### Core ideas

Wrede's questions cover:

- basic world rules
- alternate Earth scenarios
- physical geography
- climate
- history
- peoples and customs
- government
- commerce
- occupations
- education
- medicine
- daily life
- transportation
- trade
- social mobility
- religion
- magic/technology analogues

Crucially, Wrede says writers **do not need to answer every question**. The purpose is to reveal where setting assumptions do or do not hang together.

#### Glasspunk takeaways

Use this as a periodic **region and faction audit**, not a form to complete.

For each Glasspunk region ask:

- Why did settlement occur here?
- What resource or infrastructure makes the region important?
- What does it import and export?
- What occupations dominate?
- Which occupations have high prestige?
- What environmental limits affect ordinary life?
- What technology changed the region most?
- Who owns important land/infrastructure/data?
- What historical event remains politically contentious?
- Which neighboring region is partner, rival, or dependency?

---

### Mark Rosenfelder — *The Planet Construction Kit*

**Type:** Broad construction reference  
**Priority:** Medium; useful as a lookup reference  
**Access:** Full book via purchase/library; free companion material and contents online  
https://www.zompist.com/pck.html

#### Core ideas

Rosenfelder works through causal relationships among:

- astronomy and planetary conditions
- geology
- climate
- biology
- history
- culture
- government
- economics
- religion
- technology
- warfare
- mapping and modeling

#### Glasspunk takeaways

Because Glasspunk retains Earth physics and geography, the planetary-construction material is lower priority.

Use the book primarily to check:

- climate consequences
- biology/ecology
- technology diffusion
- economics
- institutional development
- warfare/security
- historical causality

The key principle is that technologies emerge through histories and infrastructures rather than appearing as isolated inventions.

## 4. Visual Thinking, Sketchbooks, and Art Direction

### Jeff VanderMeer — *Wonderbook: The Illustrated Guide to Creating Imaginative Fiction*

**Type:** Visual writing/worldbuilding manual  
**Priority:** Very high for Glasspunk's atlas/sketchbook approach  
**Access:** Full book via library/purchase; previews online  
Publisher information:  
https://www.abramsbooks.com/product/wonderbook-revised-and-expanded_9781613124635/

#### Core ideas

*Wonderbook* treats diagrams, drawings, photographs, associative thinking, and nonlinear exploration as genuine tools of fiction development.

It includes extensive illustrations and material on:

- imagination
- characterization
- worldbuilding
- story ecosystems
- exposition
- narrative design
- scenes
- revision

#### Glasspunk takeaways

Maintain a **working visual sketchbook** separate from polished canon.

Include:

- hand sketches
- maps
- machinery
- glass forms
- infrastructure diagrams
- insect anatomy
- botanical drawings
- signage
- advertisements
- clothing
- photographs of real Toledo/NW Ohio
- materials and textures
- transit diagrams
- AI concept thumbnails
- political graphics
- newspaper fragments
- reactor/data-center/wetland sections

AI-generated images should function as **exploratory thumbnails and references**, not automatically as finished illustrations.

---

### Jared Blando — *How to Draw Fantasy Art and RPG Maps*

**Type:** Practical illustrated mapmaking  
**Priority:** Medium-high for developing a human-authored map aesthetic  
**Access:** Book/library; publisher previews and some library ebook access  
Publisher:  
https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/631201/how-to-draw-fantasy-art-and-rpg-maps-by-jared-blando/

#### Core ideas

Step-by-step mapmaking methods covering:

- terrain symbols
- boundaries
- roads
- settlements
- landmarks
- map composition
- manual and digital drawing approaches

#### Glasspunk takeaways

The goal is not to invent the underlying geography; real geographic data can do that.

Use map-drawing skills to convert analytical geography into an authored visual form:

**real data → Python/R spatial simplification → printable geometry → manual redraw/trace → scan → restrained digital cleanup**

Possible Glasspunk visual language:

- monochrome/two-color maps
- uneven ink
- wetland symbols
- simplified coastlines
- landmark silhouettes
- engineering annotation
- hand lettering or carefully controlled typography
- limited ornament

This can substantially reduce the “generic AI concept art” appearance.

## 5. Fiction-to-World Translation and Narrative Economy

### Brandon Sanderson — BYU Worldbuilding Lectures

**Type:** Free practical lectures  
**Priority:** High  
**Access:** Free through official/public lecture recordings  
YouTube channel/course materials are searchable through Brandon Sanderson's official online presence.

#### Core ideas

Useful principles include:

- iceberg model of worldbuilding
- reader learning curves
- physical versus cultural setting
- avoiding exposition conversations where characters explain facts they already know
- creating depth through carefully selected implications
- developing a few distinctive ideas deeply instead of dozens superficially

#### Glasspunk takeaways

**Model widely. Narrate narrowly.**

The Glasspunk knowledge base may track:

- carbon/nitrogen/phosphorus
- energy and compute
- hydrology
- climate
- insect dynamics
- population
- AI
- surveillance
- biotechnology

A short story should normally reveal only the subset that matters to the protagonist's immediate problem.

---

### *Kobold Guide to Worldbuilding*

**Type:** Essay collection by professional RPG/world designers  
**Priority:** Medium initially; high if Glasspunk becomes a game setting  
**Access:** Commercial book/PDF; publisher information online  
https://koboldpress.com/kpstore/product/kobold-guide-to-worldbuilding/

#### Core ideas

Topics include:

- setting design
- inside-out versus outside-in approaches
- history
- maps
- societies
- cities and nations
- culture
- technology
- religion
- guilds
- shared settings
- world bibles
- avoiding “kitchen sink” world design

#### Glasspunk takeaways

Game worldbuilding emphasizes a useful question:

> **What can characters actually do here?**

A region is not complete merely because it has history and visual identity.

It needs action possibilities.

Example — Black Swamp:

- scientific expeditions
- contested restoration work
- dangerous terrain
- biosecurity failures
- smuggling
- jurisdiction conflicts
- missing sensors
- invasive species
- sabotage
- wildlife events

Example — Energy Coast:

- grid emergencies
- cyberattacks
- reactor maintenance
- data-center failures
- labor disputes
- espionage
- cooling-water conflicts
- transmission sabotage

Also useful as a warning against putting every speculative technology into the center of the same story.

## 6. The Essential Corrective: Do Not Overbuild

### M. John Harrison — essays on worldbuilding

**Type:** Critical essays / warning against excessive worldbuilding  
**Priority:** Very high as an editorial counterweight  
**Access:** Short essays available freely on Harrison's blog

“I’m not against worldbuilding…”  
https://ambientehotel.wordpress.com/2012/04/19/im-not-against-worldbuilding/

Related worldbuilding posts are available through the same site.

#### Core ideas

Harrison argues against worlds that become **over-engineered and under-designed**.

He favors:

- economy
- compression
- implication
- ambiguity
- negative space
- reader participation

He objects to excessive “authentication” in which authors keep explaining their worlds in an effort to prove that they are believable.

#### Glasspunk takeaways

The computational model can know far more than the fiction says.

A Python model might know the full land-cover history behind a restored shoreline.

A story may need only:

> The old marina was marsh again.

That is enough if the surrounding story makes the world credible.

Harrison is particularly important for Glasspunk because a technically inclined setting can easily become an illustrated research report rather than fiction.

## 10. A Synthesized Glasspunk Method

The strongest combined methodology from these references is:

### Tolkien — Coherence

Build causal rules. A speculative change should produce consequences.

### Wolf — Architecture

Maintain the world independently of individual stories and media.

### Le Guin — Culture

Show the world through everyday work, relationships, artifacts, language, memory, and place.

### Hergenrader — Structure

Define governance, economics, social relationships, and cultural forces before filling the world with lore.

### Hergenrader & Slota — Simulation

Change assumptions, model consequences, and test institutions through scenarios and role-play.

### Wrede — Audit

Use questions to find missing causal relationships and inconsistencies.

### VanderMeer — Visual Discovery

Use drawings, diagrams, maps, photographs, and experimental imagery as tools for discovering the world.

### Rosenfelder — Causal Reference

Check physical, biological, economic, and technological consequences.

### Sanderson — Narrative Economy

Model widely but narrate narrowly.

### Kobold — Action

Ask what characters can actually do in each region.

### Blando — Authored Visual Style

Develop enough manual cartographic skill that maps feel designed rather than generated.

### Harrison — Restraint

Leave some things unexplained. The world model should be more explicit than the story.

## 11. Recommended Working Formula

For Glasspunk:

**research → model → extrapolate → map/sketch → inhabit → write → discover contradictions → revise → write again**

Avoid:

**map → encyclopedia → more lore → more lore → never write the story**

A practical cycle might be:

1. Select one real Glasspunk location.
2. Document its 2020s baseline.
3. Select one or two future drivers.
4. Model plausible consequences.
5. Identify groups that benefit or lose.
6. Create a character caught between those interests.
7. Make a rough map or sketch.
8. Write a 1,000–4,000 word proof-of-concept story.
9. Record contradictions revealed by the story.
10. Update the world bible and canon.
11. Repeat in another region.

---

## 12. Glasspunk Reference Principle

> **The world should be deep enough to generate consequences, coherent enough to withstand scrutiny, culturally rich enough to feel inhabited, visually distinctive enough to be remembered, and incomplete enough that the reader can still imagine beyond the page.**
